The Dutch Tulip Mania: When a Flower Ruled the Market

荷兰郁金香狂热：当一株花统治了市场

导读：一株郁金香球茎，曾在 17 世纪的荷兰贵过工匠十年的收入，又在短短几个月内暴跌九成，让无数家庭破产。这被称为人类历史上第一个有记录的“金融泡沫”。它因何而起，又为何崩塌？读完本文，你将理解投机、供需与恐慌如何共同塑造了现代经济中反复出现的泡沫与危机。

A

Few flowers are so closely bound up with a nation's identity as the tulip is with the Netherlands. Ever since the first bulbs arrived from the Ottoman Empire in the mid-sixteenth century, tulips have occupied a remarkable place in Dutch culture and history. So intense was the national passion for the flower during the Dutch Golden Age that a tulip bulb bubble — commonly known as "Tulip Mania", a phrase coined by later historians — eventually took hold. Widely regarded as the first recorded financial bubble in history, the Tulip Mania of 1636–1637 was an episode in which bulb prices were driven by speculators to extraordinary heights before the market collapsed, plunging the Dutch economy into a severe crisis that echoed for years. Economists still cite the affair as the significant example of how quickly a widespread craze can occur when speculation involves itself in an ordinary commodity.

A

很少有哪种花像郁金香之于荷兰那样，与一个国家的身份紧密相连。自 16 世纪中叶第一批球茎从奥斯曼帝国运抵以来，郁金香就在荷兰的文化与历史中占据着非凡地位。荷兰黄金时代国人对这种花的狂热如此强烈，以至于一场“郁金香球茎泡沫”——俗称“郁金香狂热”，这个说法由后世历史学家创造——最终形成。郁金香狂热（1636–1637 年）被广泛认为是历史上第一个有记录的金融泡沫：在那场事件中，投机者把球茎价格推至惊人的高度，随后市场崩溃，荷兰经济被拖入一场持续多年的严重危机。经济学家至今仍把此事引为典型：当投机卷入一种普通商品时，一场广泛的狂热会传播得多么迅速。

B

The Dutch Golden Age, roughly spanning the seventeenth century, transformed a modest seafaring nation into one of the most affluent societies in Europe. The port city of Amsterdam became a dominant centre of international trade, where merchants exchanged goods from every corner of the globe. Chartered trading companies grew into powerful institutions, managing the nation's commerce with Indonesia and other distant lands and generating immense wealth for the city's investors, who pooled their funds into new ventures. Amsterdam's booming economy nourished a remarkable flourishing of the arts — the paintings of Rembrandt and Vermeer remain among its most precious legacies — and fostered a thriving market for luxury commodities such as blue glass, fine china and oriental carpets. The influx of silver from trade routes steadily expanded the money supply, fuelling growth in nearly every industry, from shipbuilding to printing.

B

荷兰黄金时代大致贯穿 17 世纪，把一个不起眼的航海小国变成了欧洲最富裕的社会之一。港口城市阿姆斯特丹成为国际贸易的核心，商人在这里交换来自世界各地的货物。持特许状的贸易公司成长为强大的机构，管理着荷兰与印尼等遥远地区的商贸往来，为城市的投资者创造了巨大财富，他们又把资金投入新的冒险事业。阿姆斯特丹繁荣的经济滋养了艺术的辉煌——伦勃朗和维米尔的作品至今仍是最珍贵的遗产——也培育了一个繁荣的奢侈品市场：蓝玻璃、精美瓷器和东方地毯都是抢手货。贸易路线上白银的大量涌入扩大了货币供应，推动从造船到印刷的几乎所有行业增长。

C

Trade with foreign lands brought exotic goods to Europe that had never been seen before, and the tulip — not native to Europe — was among the most striking arrivals. The story begins in the Ottoman Empire, where a sultan sent bulbs and seeds to Vienna; shortly after 1554, these seeds reached Amsterdam, and their popularity began to rise, prompting rich collectors to seek them out. A botanical study in 1593 revealed that tulips could withstand the harsh northern-European climate, which further boosted their desirability. With their intensely colourful petals, tulips resembled no other flower then popular in Europe, and growing them in one's garden rapidly became an important status symbol — a visible demonstration of wealth and taste. Yet the flower remained scarce: because each bulb took years to flower and could be cultivated only in small numbers, supply could never keep pace with demand, a fundamental condition that economists would later identify as the seed of every bubble.

C

与异域的贸易把欧洲人从未见过的奇珍带回欧洲，郁金香——并非欧洲本土植物——就是其中最引人注目的来客之一。故事始于奥斯曼帝国：一位苏丹把球茎和种子送往维也纳；1554 年后不久，这些种子到达阿姆斯特丹，人气开始上升，促使富有的收藏家争相求购。1593 年的一项植物学研究揭示，郁金香能够耐受北欧的严寒气候，这进一步抬升了它们的受欢迎度。凭借浓烈多彩的花瓣，郁金香与当时欧洲流行的任何花卉都迥然不同，在自家庭院种上郁金香迅速成为一种重要的身份象征——财富与品位的直观展示。然而这种花始终稀缺：由于每颗球茎要多年才开花，且只能少量栽培，供给永远赶不上需求——经济学家后来把这一根本条件认定为一切泡沫的种子。

D

Tulips, a hardy species of perennial plant, reproduce through bulbs: a bulb planted in the ground may not send down roots and flower until seven to twelve years later. Between April and May, each plant blooms for about one week, while new bulbs appear between June and September, confining Dutch sales to that short season. Because the window was so narrow, a rudimentary derivatives market — similar to modern stock markets with their options and futures contracts — gradually arose, allowing traders to buy and sell tulips all year round. Buyers and sellers signed contracts before a notary, agreeing to future purchases at fixed prices, and this lively contract market became an integral part of the booming Dutch tulip industry. In effect, the market provided a range of financial tools that required little more than confidence in tomorrow's prices.

D

郁金香是一种耐寒的多年生植物，靠球茎繁殖：一颗种入土中的球茎可能要七到十二年后才生根开花。每年四到五月间，每株开花约一周，而新球茎在六到九月间出现，荷兰的销售因此被限制在短短一个季节内。正因为窗口期太短，一个初级的衍生品市场——类似现代的股票市场及其期权和期货合约——逐渐形成，让交易者可以全年买卖郁金香。买卖双方在公证人面前签约，约定未来按固定价格成交；这个活跃的合约市场成为蓬勃发展的荷兰郁金香产业不可或缺的一部分。实际上，这个市场提供了一系列金融工具，而它们所需要的，不过是人们对未来价格的一点信心。

E

As the market matured, tulips were classified into groups and priced according to their rarity. In general, solid-coloured tulips were worth less than those with multiple colours. Couleren described solid-coloured red, white or yellow blooms; rosen referred to multi-coloured varieties, often red, pink or white; and violetten denoted white tulips bearing purple or lilac markings. The most coveted of all were the bizarden, tulips with a yellow background and striking red, brown or purple colouration. Curiously, bulbs infected with a benign mosaic virus — which caused dramatic "flames" of colour to appear on the petals — sold at a premium precisely because their unusual beauty made them rare and hence more valuable. In contrast, the commonest varieties remained cheap enough for ordinary citizens, a distinct trait that would soon be transformed by rising speculation.

E

随着市场走向成熟，郁金香被分门别类，按稀有程度定价。一般来说，纯色的郁金香不如多色的值钱。Couleren 指纯红、纯白或纯黄的品种；rosen 指多色品种，常为红、粉或白色；violetten 则指带有紫或淡紫色斑纹的白郁金香。最受追捧的是 bizarden：黄色底、带有醒目的红、棕或紫色花纹。奇怪的是，感染了良性花叶病毒的球茎——病毒让花瓣上出现戏剧性的“火焰”色斑——反而以溢价出售，正因为它们独特的美使其稀有，因而更值钱。相比之下，最普通的品种仍然便宜得连普通市民都买得起——这一鲜明特征很快就被不断升温的投机改变了。

F

Tulip prices rose steadily as demand swelled, and bulbs were purchased at ever higher prices by speculators who planned to resell them at a profit, much like modern-day house "flippers." Between 1634 and 1637, an index of Dutch tulip prices soared from roughly one guilder per bulb to a lofty sixty guilders. Successful traders reinvested their gains in new bulbs and contracts, and merchants sold their property — homes, land, even dowries — to acquire a handful of bulbs, hoping to earn more in a single season than in a lifetime of trade. As the bubble swelled, already expensive bulbs experienced a twentyfold price surge within a single month. By the peak of the mania in February 1637, a single tulip bulb was worth roughly ten times a craftsman's annual income — a considerable sum that even the most rational observer found hard to perceive as justified.

F

随着需求膨胀，郁金香价格稳步上涨，投机者以越来越高的价格买进球茎，打算转手获利，就像今天的“炒房客”一样。1634 至 1637 年间，荷兰郁金香价格指数从每颗约一荷兰盾飙升至高昂的六十荷兰盾。成功的交易者把收益再投资于新球茎和合约；商人们变卖家产——房屋、土地、甚至嫁妆——只为买上几颗球茎，盼望一个季节赚到一生经商都赚不到的钱。随着泡沫膨胀，本就昂贵的球茎在短短一个月内经历了二十倍的价格暴涨。到 1637 年 2 月狂热顶峰时，一颗郁金香球茎大约相当于工匠十年的收入——即便最理性的观察者也很难认为这样一笔巨款是合理的。

G

The reckoning came swiftly. In early February 1637, a trader failed to appear at an auction, and confidence evaporated overnight. Within days, bulb prices plummeted, and panic swept through the market as buyers refused to honour contracts signed at absurd prices. Merchants who had borrowed heavily to speculate found themselves unable to repay their debts; many were driven into bankruptcy, and the broader Dutch economy slid into a recession that lingered for years. Domestic authorities prohibited the most reckless contracts and restricted further speculation, but by then the damage was done. Although the crash did not destroy the Dutch Golden Age as a whole, it delivered a brutal lesson about the dangers of speculative excess, and the episode was remembered — and studied — for centuries.

G

清算来得很快。1637 年 2 月初，一名交易者没有出现在拍卖会上，信心一夜之间蒸发。几天之内，球茎价格暴跌，恐慌席卷市场，买家拒绝履行以荒唐价格签订的合约。那些借了重金投机的商人发现自己无力偿还债务，许多人破产，整个荷兰经济滑入一场持续数年的衰退。当局禁止了最不计后果的合约，并限制进一步的投机，但那时损失已经造成。虽然这场崩溃并没有摧毁整个荷兰黄金时代，但它给投机过度的危险上了一堂残酷的课，这起事件被后人铭记并研究了数百年。

H

Why does Tulip Mania still fascinate economists today? Because it contains, in miniature, every ingredient of every financial bubble that has followed: a novel product that captures the public imagination, easy credit, rising prices that attract more buyers, and a final collapse driven by fear rather than reason. From the South Sea Bubble of the eighteenth century to the dot-com mania and the housing crisis of the twenty-first, the same pattern recurs. Modern scholars attribute the pattern to a failure of confidence, and recognize that markets require rules and transparency to sustain themselves, whereas unchecked speculation tends to end in tears. The tulip itself, meanwhile, remains a beloved flower — but the cautionary tale it left behind is one that every investor would do well to remember: when value is driven by speculation rather than intrinsic worth, what goes up must eventually come down.

H

为什么郁金香狂热至今仍让经济学家着迷？因为它以缩影形式囊括了此后每一场金融泡沫的全部要素：一个抓住公众想象力的新奇产品、宽松的信贷、吸引更多买家入场的上涨价格，以及一场由恐惧而非理性驱动的最终崩盘。从 18 世纪的南海泡沫，到互联网泡沫和 21 世纪的次贷危机，同样的模式一再重演。当代学者把这种模式归因于信心的丧失，并认识到市场需要规则和透明度来维持自身，而无节制的投机往往以眼泪收场。与此同时，郁金香本身依然是受人喜爱的花卉——但它留下的警示故事，是每个投资者都该牢记的：当价值由投机而非内在价值驱动时，涨上去的终将落下来。
