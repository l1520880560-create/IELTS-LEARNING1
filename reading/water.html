Water: The Urban Supply Puzzle

城市供水 · 一座城市的生命线

1

Most of us turn on a tap without a thought, yet the water that arrives clean and safe has travelled through one of the most complex systems ever built. The puzzle of urban water begins with a paradox: the earth is covered in water, but less than one percent of it is fresh, accessible and fit for human use. Rivers and lakes hold only a tiny fraction; most fresh water lies hidden beneath the surface as groundwater, or frozen in ice caps and glaciers. Cities therefore cannot simply rely on the water around them. They must capture it, store it, treat it and deliver it — a four-stage process that every urban civilisation has had to solve in its own way.

1

我们中的大多数人打开水龙头时不会多想，可这干净安全的水，来自人类建造过的最复杂的系统之一。城市供水的难题始于一个悖论：地球表面覆盖着水，但可供人类使用的淡水不到百分之一。河流湖泊只占极小一部分；大部分淡水藏在地表之下，成为地下水，或冻结在冰盖和冰川里。因此城市不能简单地依赖周边的水。它必须取水、存水、净水、送水——每一个城市文明都必须以自己的方式解决这四个环节。

2

The earliest cities solved the puzzle with engineering on an impressive scale. The Romans built aqueducts to carry water from mountain springs across valleys and hills, channel it through lead pipes into public baths and fountains, and drain waste through sewers into the river. The water that reached the city was abundant but not always clean; rich citizens could afford private supplies, while the poor depend on public fountains. The system worked for centuries because the city's population remained small and its sources lay upriver, far from the contamination of its own waste. When both of those conditions failed, so did the water.

2

最早的城市用规模惊人的工程技术解决了这个难题。罗马人修建输水道，把山泉引过山谷和丘陵，用铅管送入公共浴场和喷泉，再用下水道把污水排入河流。到达城市的水量充足，却未必干净；富有的市民买得起私人供水，穷人则依赖公共喷泉。这套系统运转了几个世纪，因为城市人口不多，水源又在远离自身污染的上游。当这两个条件失效时，供水系统也就失灵了。

3

The industrial city inherited this ancient model and stretched it to breaking point. As factories and tenements crowded along the rivers, the same water served as supply, transport and sewer; the Thames became an open drain, and cholera spread through the city's wells with terrifying speed. The turning point came in London in the 1850s, when a physician traced the outbreak to a single contaminated pump, and the authorities finally accepted that clean water and safe sanitation were public responsibilities, not private luxuries. The city invested in a network of sewers, filters and reservoirs, and for the first time the supply of water became a system rather than an accident.

3

工业城市继承了这套古老模式，并把它拉伸到极限。当工厂和廉租房沿河挤满时，同一条河既是水源、又是航道、还是下水道；泰晤士河成了一条露天排水沟，霍乱顺着城里的水井以惊人的速度蔓延。转折点出现在 1850 年代的伦敦：一位医生把疫情追溯到一口被污染的公共水井，当局终于承认，干净的饮用水和安全的卫生设施是公共责任，而不是私人奢侈品。城市投资建设了庞大的下水道网络、过滤池和水库，供水第一次成为一套体系，而非偶然。

4

The modern urban system that grew from this crisis has four components: sources, treatment, storage and distribution. Water is abstracted from rivers or reservoirs behind dams, pumped to a treatment plant where it is settled, filtered and disinfected with chlorine, then stored in elevated tanks and distributed through a network of mains and pipes to every tap. The capacity of each part must be matched to demand, because water cannot be manufactured on the spot: what is not stored in the wet season is simply lost, and what is not purified before delivery may poison the people who drink it. Reliability — not price — is the real product of the water industry.

4

从这场危机中成长起来的现代城市供水系统有四个环节：水源、处理、储存和配送。水从河流或大坝后的水库中抽取，泵送到水处理厂，在那里沉淀、过滤、加氯消毒，再存入高位水箱，通过干管和支管网络送到每一个水龙头。每一部分的容量都必须与需求匹配，因为水无法现产现用：雨季不储存就会白白流走，送水前不净化就可能毒害饮用者。可靠性——而不是价格——才是供水业真正出售的产品。

5

Agriculture, not cities, is the largest consumer of fresh water. Irrigation accounts for about seventy percent of all water use worldwide, and the efficiency of that irrigation decides how much of the supply is wasted. Traditional flood irrigation loses most of the water to evaporation and runoff; modern drip systems deliver water directly to the roots and cut use by half or more. The choice matters enormously: a city downstream may depend on the very river that farmers divert for their fields, and when upstream regions expand their irrigation, downstream cities face shortages they did not cause. Water disputes cross borders, and the same river can serve a dozen nations at once.

5

农业而非城市，才是淡水最大的消费者。灌溉约占全球用水量的七成，而灌溉效率决定了有多少供给被浪费。传统漫灌把大部分水耗散于蒸发和径流；现代滴灌把水直接送到根部，用水量可减少一半以上。这个选择关系重大：下游城市可能恰恰依赖农民从河道引走的那条河；当上游地区扩大灌溉时，下游城市就要面对并非自己造成的短缺。水事纠纷跨越国界，同一条河可能同时服务于十几个国家。

6

When surface water is not enough, cities turn to the ground. Groundwater lies in aquifers — layers of sand and rock that hold water like a sponge — and for a century it has supplied a growing share of urban demand. But the pumping often exceeds the rate at which rain recharge the aquifer, and the water table sinks year by year. In some coastal cities the salt water creeps inland to fill the space left behind, ruining the well; in others the ground itself subsides as the water is removed. Over-extraction of groundwater is a silent crisis: invisible, gradual and extremely difficult to reverse.

6

当地表水不够用时，城市转向地下。地下水储存在含水层——像海绵一样含水的砂石层——一个世纪以来，它满足了不断增长的城市需求。但抽水速度常常超过雨水补给的速度，地下水位逐年下降。在一些沿海城市，海水向内陆渗入，填补被抽空的空间，毁掉了水井；在另一些地方，地下水被抽走后地表本身发生沉降。地下水超采是一场无声的危机：看不见、渐进，且极难逆转。

7

Wastewater is finally being recognised as a resource rather than a problem. Modern treatment plants remove solids and nutrients, and the treated water can be released into rivers, used for industry, or reused directly for irrigation and even drinking. The standards are strict: water that is safe to discharge into a river may not be safe to drink, and the difference is measured in parts per million. Yet the benefits are large — recycling spares the rivers, reduces the demand for new sources, and lowers the cost of water for everyone. Singapore, Israel and California now treat almost all their wastewater, proving that a city can grow without consuming more water.

7

污水终于被当作资源而非问题来对待。现代处理厂去除固体和营养物质，处理后的水可以排入河流、供工业使用，或直接回用于灌溉甚至饮用。标准很严格：能安全排入河道的水未必能安全饮用，两者之差以百万分之一计量。但好处巨大——回用保护了河流，减少了对新水源的需求，也降低了每个人的用水成本。新加坡、以色列和加利福尼亚如今处理几乎全部污水，证明一座城市可以在不消耗更多水的情况下继续增长。

8

The future of urban water depends on the same four steps, applied more intelligently. Sensors and smart meters can detect leaks and identify waste; pricing can encourage conservation; and desalination — turning sea water into fresh water — can supplement the supply of coastal cities, though at a high energy cost. The deeper lesson is that water is not a product like any other: it is a common good that connects farms, factories and families, and its management requires planning, cooperation and long-term thinking. A city that secures its water secures its future; a city that neglect it may find that the taps run dry, regardless of how many dams it builds or how much money it spends.

8

城市供水的未来仍取决于这四个环节，只是运用得更聪明。传感器和智能水表能发现漏损、识别浪费；阶梯定价能鼓励节水；海水淡化——把海水变成淡水——可以为沿海城市补充供给，尽管能源成本高昂。更深层的教训是：水不是普通商品，它是连接农场、工厂和家庭的公共品，管理水需要规划、合作和长远思考。一座守住了水的城市就守住了未来；一座忽视水的城市，无论建多少大坝、花多少钱，都可能发现水龙头干涸。
