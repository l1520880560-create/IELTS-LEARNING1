The Sydney Opera House: A Monument to Bold Ambition

1

The Sydney Opera House is one of the most recognisable buildings in the world — its white shell roofs, perched on a harbour peninsula, appear on postcards, films and logos in almost every country. Yet the story behind the landmark is far stranger than its image suggests. What began as an ambitious dream became a legend of cost overruns, political battles and engineering nightmares; the architect who imagined it never saw it finished, and the building itself was almost abandoned more than once. The Opera House is a monument not only to music but to the risk of thinking too big — and to the courage required to see an idea through.

1

悉尼歌剧院是世界上最容易辨认的建筑之一——它那白色的壳顶，栖居在港湾的半岛上，出现在几乎每个国家的明信片、电影和标志里。然而，这座地标背后的故事远比它的形象更离奇。它始于一个雄心勃勃的梦，却成了成本超支、政治斗争和工程噩梦的传奇；想象出它的建筑师从未见到它完工，而这座建筑本身也曾不止一次差点被放弃。歌剧院不仅是音乐的纪念碑，也是想得太大的风险的纪念碑——以及把想法坚持到底所需要的勇气的纪念碑。

2

In 1956 the government of New South Wales launched an international competition to design a new opera house on Bennelong Point, a prominent site jutting into Sydney Harbour. Entries flood in from thirty-two countries — over two hundred and thirty designs in all. The jury, led by the Finnish architect Eero Saarinen, rejected the safe, conventional submissions and selected the boldest of all: the entry of the little-known Danish architect Jørn Utzon. His drawings showed a cluster of soaring concrete shells resembling billowing sails. The judges declared that his design was so original that it could become one of the great buildings of the world — though no one was sure it could be built.

2

1956年，新南威尔士州政府发起一场国际竞赛，为伸入悉尼港的显要地块贝尼朗角设计一座新歌剧院。参赛作品从32个国家涌来，总共超过230份设计。由芬兰建筑师埃罗·沙里宁领导的评审团拒绝了稳妥而常规的提交作品，选中了最大胆的一份：鲜为人知的丹麦建筑师约恩·伍重的方案。他的图纸显示了一簇高耸的混凝土壳顶，仿佛鼓满的风帆。评委们宣称，他的设计如此原创，足以成为世界最伟大的建筑之一——尽管没人确定它能被建出来。

3

The gap between drawing and building proved enormous. The shells — each a fragment of a perfect sphere — were to be formed from precast concrete ribs, yet no engineer could at first calculate how the curved roofs could stand or how their weight could be transferred to the podium below. Utzon and the Danish engineering firm spent three years in trial and error, developing the geometry that finally made construction possible. The solution adopted — every shell cut from the same imaginary sphere — allowed the ribs to be prefabricated and lifted into place. It was a breakthrough that turned an impossible sketch into a buildable structure.

3

图纸与建造之间的鸿沟被证明是巨大的。那些壳顶——每一个都是完美球体的一部分——要用预制混凝土肋建造，但起初没有一位工程师能算出弯曲的屋顶如何立得住，重量如何传递到下面的基座。伍重与丹麦工程公司花了三年时间反复试验，最终发展出使建造成为可能的几何方案。被采用的解法——每一个壳体都从同一个想象中的球体切出——使得肋骨可以预制并吊装就位。这是一次突破，把一个不可能的画稿变成了可建造的结构。

4

Construction began in 1959 and was supposed to take four years. It would stretch to fourteen. The podium was anchored into the harbour with over five hundred concrete piers, each driven deep into the mud of the bay. As the shells rose above the water, the project consumed concrete, steel and money at a fearful rate. The roof tiles alone — over a million glossy white and cream tiles imported from Sweden — required a special adhesive developed for the harbour's salt air. By the mid-1960s the cost had exceed the original budget many times over, and the end was still nowhere in sight.

4

施工于1959年开始，原计划用四年。结果拖了十四年。基座被锚固进港湾，用了五百多根混凝土桩，每根都深深打入海湾的淤泥。随着壳顶升出水面，工程以可怕的速度消耗着混凝土、钢材和金钱。仅屋顶瓦片——超过一百万块从瑞典进口的乳白色亮面瓷砖——就需要一种为港湾盐蚀空气专门开发的特殊粘合剂。到1960年代中期，成本已超出最初预算许多倍，而终点仍遥遥无期。

5

The financial pressure soon turned political. The state government, under a new minister, grew impatient with the architect's demands and his refusal to compromise on the interior — in particular the acoustic requirements of the main hall. In 1966, amid a bitter public argument over costs, Utzon resigned and left the country, declaring he would never return. The shells were finished, but the interiors were completed by a local team that replaced the architect's plans with more conventional solutions. For decades the building carry the scars of that rupture: the grand hall was criticised for its inferior acoustics and awkward sightlines.

5

财务压力很快变成了政治压力。州政府在新部长的领导下，对建筑师坚持主厅声学要求、拒绝妥协的态度越来越不耐烦。1966年，在一场关于成本的激烈公开争吵中，伍重辞职离开了这个国家，宣称再也不回来。壳顶完工了，但内部由一支本地团队完成，他们用更常规的解决方案取代了建筑师的计划。几十年来，这座建筑都带着那次决裂的伤疤：主厅因逊色的声学和别扭的视线而受到批评。

6

The Opera House was finally opened by Queen Elizabeth II in 1973, at a final cost more than fourteen times the original estimate. Critics called it a triumph of form over function; supporters called it a masterpiece. Whatever the verdict, the building demonstrate something rare: a structure that people photograph more often than they use, yet which shapes the identity of an entire city. Its curved roofs, visible from miles away, gave Sydney — and Australia — an image that money could not have bought. The Opera House became a symbol of what a society can achieve when it dare to pay for genius.

6

歌剧院最终于1973年由伊丽莎白二世女王揭幕，最终成本是最初估算的十四倍多。批评者称它是形式压过功能的胜利；支持者称它是杰作。无论评判如何，这座建筑都展示了一种罕见的东西：一座人们拍照的频率超过使用频率、却塑造了整座城市身份的结构。它那弯曲的屋顶，数英里外就能看见，给了悉尼——也给了澳大利亚——一个金钱买不到的形象。歌剧院成了一个社会敢于为天才付钱时所能成就的象征。

7

Today the building is far more than a theatre. It houses five performance halls, restaurants and a vibrant programme of concerts, opera and festivals; its forecourt and steps have become a public gathering place for the city. In 2007 it was listed as a World Heritage site, one of the youngest buildings ever to receive the honour. UNESCO described it as "a great urban sculpture set in a magnificent waterscape", praising both the design and the engineering that made it possible. The listing marked a quiet reversal: the structure once attacked as reckless was now celebrated as one of the finest achievements of the twentieth century.

7

如今，这座建筑远不止是一座剧院。它容纳五个演出厅、餐厅和充满活力的音乐会、歌剧与节日节目；它的前院和台阶已成为城市的公共集会场所。2007年，它被列为世界遗产地，是有史以来获此殊荣的最年轻的建筑之一。联合国教科文组织称它为"置于壮丽水景中的伟大城市雕塑"，同时赞扬了成就它的设计与工程。这一名录标志着一个安静的逆转：曾被抨击为鲁莽的结构，如今被颂扬为二十世纪最杰出的成就之一。

8

The lesson of the Opera House resonates far beyond architecture. It reminds us that the greatest risk in any project is often the refusal to take a risk; that costs are easier to measure than value; and that patience with a difficult idea can yield rewards no committee could have predicted. The shells still rise above the harbour, as fresh as they were in 1956, and the building still perform its true function — not only hosting opera, but reminding us that a society gains most when it refuses to build only what it already knows how to build.

8

歌剧院的教训远远超出建筑领域。它提醒我们，任何项目最大的风险往往正是拒绝冒险；成本比价值更容易衡量；而对一个困难想法保持耐心，能收获任何委员会都预测不到的回报。那些壳顶依然耸立在港湾上空，与1956年一样崭新，这座建筑也仍在履行它真正的职能——不只是上演歌剧，更提醒我们：一个社会收获最大的时刻，是它拒绝只建造已知如何建造之物的时候。
