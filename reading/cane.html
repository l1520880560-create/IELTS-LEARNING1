Cane Toads in Australia: A Cautionary Tale of Invasion

如何用一百年时间把一场害虫防治变成生态灾难

导读：当人类试图用另一种生物来解决问题时，结果常常出人意料。1935 年，澳大利亚为保护甘蔗田引入了一种来自中美洲的蟾蜍，如今它已蔓延到半个大陆，成为教科书级的入侵物种案例。

1

In 1935, farmers in the cane fields of Queensland were fighting a chronic battle against the Greyback beetle, whose larvae eroded the roots of sugarcane plants and impaired the harvest. Desperate for a swift solution, the sugar industry turned to an artificial remedy: a tropical toad from Central America that was said to devour beetles in enormous numbers. The plan seemed beneficial and feasible in theory, yet nobody could foresee the calamity that the introduction would unleash. Today scientists cite the cane toad as a classic witness to the risks of biological control — a lesson in how ignorance and good intentions can combine to produce catastrophic harm.

1

1935 年，昆士兰州甘蔗田里的农民正在与灰背甲虫进行一场旷日持久的战争：甲虫的幼虫啃食甘蔗的根系，破坏收成。急于找到快速解决办法的制糖业求助于一种人造手段——一种来自中美洲的热带蟾蜍，据说能吞食大量甲虫。这个计划在理论上似乎有益且可行，然而没有人能预见这次引进会释放怎样的灾难。如今，科学家把甘蔗蟾蜍当作生物防治风险的一个经典见证——一个关于无知与善意如何结合、酿成灾难性后果的教训。

2

The cane toad resembles a giant native frog, but appearances are deceiving. Behind its dull brown skin lie two glands that secrete a milky toxin called bufotoxin, powerful enough to induce cardiac arrest in many predators. In its native habitat of Central and South America, predators had evolved innate immunity or learned to avoid the toad; the toxin posed little threat to a stable ecosystem. Australia, however, had no such protection. Native quolls, goannas, snakes and even freshwater crocodiles had never encounter the poison before. When they attacked the intruder, the toad's toxin would paralyse them within minutes, and mortality rates among predatory species plummeted.

2

甘蔗蟾蜍看上去像一只巨大的本地蛙，但外表具有欺骗性。在暗淡的棕色皮肤下，是两只能分泌乳白色毒素（蟾蜍毒素）的腺体，其毒性足以让许多捕食者心脏骤停。在中美洲和南美洲的原生栖息地里，捕食者已经进化出先天的免疫力，或学会了避开蟾蜍；毒素对这种稳定的生态系统威胁不大。然而澳大利亚没有这样的保护。本土的袋鼬、巨蜥、蛇乃至淡水鳄鱼从未接触过这种毒液。当它们攻击入侵者时，蟾蜍的毒素会在几分钟内使它们瘫痪，捕食物种的死亡率急剧下降。

3

To make matters worse, the toad breeds with astonishing speed. A single female can lay more than thirty thousand eggs a year, and the tadpoles grow into adults that retain the ability to reproduce almost immediately. With no natural enemies and an abundant supply of food, the population expanded across the tropical north at a pace that accelerated decade by decade. Early surveys recorded a front line advancing about ten kilometres a year; later studies found toads settling new territory up to fifty kilometres annually. Researchers were struck by how erratically the invaders moved — some individuals were detected hundreds of kilometres ahead of the main army, carried by trucks and cargo along the roads of the outback.

3

更糟的是，蟾蜍的繁殖速度快得惊人。一只雌蟾蜍每年能产下超过三万枚卵，蝌蚪长成成体后几乎立刻就能再次繁殖。由于没有天敌、食物充足，蟾蜍群以每十年不断加快的速度席卷整个热带北部。早期的调查记录到前锋线每年推进约十公里；后来的研究发现，蟾蜍每年能占领多达五十公里的新领地。研究人员对入侵者无规律的行动方式感到震惊——一些个体被发现超出主力大军数百公里，它们是被卡车和货物沿着内陆道路带过去的。

4

Why did the toad succeed where other introductions failed? Ecologists attribute its success to a combination of factors. Its skin contains toxins that hinder most would-be predators; its diet is astonishingly broad, ranging from beetles and spiders to nests of native birds and even pet food left outdoors. Unlike sensitive native amphibians, the toad tolerates dry conditions and will burrow into soil to survive drought. Above all, it benefits from a lack of the diseases and parasites that would normally constrain its numbers back home. Freed from these checks, its genetic diversity among the founding population proved sufficiently flexible to let the species adapt to almost every environment it entered.

4

为什么蟾蜍在其他引进物种失败的地方取得了成功？生态学家将其成功归因于多种因素的组合。它的皮肤含有阻碍大多数潜在捕食者的毒素；它的食性惊人地广泛，从甲虫和蜘蛛到本土鸟类的巢，甚至户外宠物食品，无所不食。与敏感的本土两栖动物不同，蟾蜍耐受干旱，会钻入土壤以度过旱季。最重要的是，它受益于缺乏那些原本会在原产地制约其数量的疾病和寄生虫。摆脱了这些制约，奠基种群的遗传多样性被证明足够灵活，使该物种能够适应其进入的几乎每一种环境。

5

The ecological damage has been severe. Studies in the Northern Territory document sharp declines in populations of quolls, lizards and venomous snakes that once dominated the food chain. Frogs and small mammals have been disrupted as the toads consume their prey and compete with them for shelter. Even the buffer between species has thinned: scientists observed that dingoes and birds which ignored the toad's warning signals often paid with their lives. The loss of these apex predators triggered a cascade of changes, and ecologists now analyze the invasion as a natural experiment in how a single introduced species can diminish the diversity of an entire continent.

5

生态损害是严重的。北领地的研究记录到曾主导食物链的袋鼬、蜥蜴和毒蛇数量急剧下降。随着蟾蜍消耗它们的猎物并与之争夺庇护所，蛙类和小型哺乳动物受到干扰。甚至物种之间的缓冲也变薄了：科学家观察到，忽略蟾蜍警告信号的野狗和鸟类常常付出生命的代价。这些顶级捕食者的丧失引发了一连串变化，生态学家现在把这次入侵分析为一次自然实验，看一个引进物种如何削弱整个大陆的多样性。

6

In response, Australians have attempt almost every form of control. Community groups organise annual "toad busting" events in which volunteers collect the amphibians by the truckload; authorities have erected fences and set traps baited with food the toads cannot resist. Scientists have explored biological agents, including a virus specific to the toad, and genetic techniques to minimize female reproduction. Each method has its limitation: toads are fragile to poison but too numerous to catch, and any agent that kills them may accidentally harm native frogs that resemble them closely. The sheer quantity of toads — estimated in the hundreds of millions — makes eradication impossible, and managers now speak less of eliminating the toad than of learning to live with it.

6

作为回应，澳大利亚人尝试了几乎每一种控制方法。社区组织举办一年一度的"灭蟾日"活动，志愿者用卡车一车车地收集蟾蜍；当局竖起了围栏，设置了用蟾蜍无法抗拒的食物作诱饵的陷阱。科学家探索了生物手段，包括一种对蟾蜍特异的病毒，以及尽量减少雌性繁殖的基因技术。每种方法都有其局限：蟾蜍对毒药很敏感但数量太多抓不完，而任何能杀死它们的药剂都可能意外伤害与它们极为相似的本土蛙类。蟾蜍的数量——估计有数亿只——使根除变得不可能，管理者如今很少再谈消灭蟾蜍，而更多谈学会与它共存。

7

The debate over what to do next exposes deeper questions about how humans manage nature. Some argue that limited budgets should be allocated to protect the species most at risk, rather than spent on a losing battle against a superior competitor. Others emphasize that every year of effort buys time for native animals to evolve tolerance, and that abandon the fight would condemn more species to extinction. Whether the goal is control or coexistence, researchers agree on one principle: the earlier an invasion is detected, the cheaper and more effective the response. Prevention, they insist, will always outweigh cure.

7

关于下一步该做什么的争论，暴露了人类如何管理自然的更深层问题。一些人认为，有限的预算应分配给保护最濒危的物种，而不是花在一场对抗强大竞争者的必败之战上。另一些人强调，每一年的努力都为本土动物进化耐受性争取了时间，而放弃战斗将注定更多物种走向灭绝。无论目标是控制还是共存，研究人员在一个原则上达成一致：入侵越早被发现，应对就越便宜、越有效。他们坚持认为，预防永远重于补救。

8

The cane toad has become more than an ecological problem; it is a symbol of human overconfidence. Its spread across Australia demonstrates how apparently reasonable decisions, made without adequate knowledge of complex systems, can unexpectedly backfire. For students of reading and vocabulary alike, the story offers a remarkable example of cause and effect: how a single import in 1935 rippled through the decades to reshape the ecology of a continent. As scientists continue to study the toad's remarkable adaptability, one lesson stands out — humility before the natural world, and the wisdom of measuring twice before cutting once.

8

甘蔗蟾蜍已经不仅仅是一个生态问题，它是人类过度自信的象征。它在澳大利亚的蔓延表明，表面合理的决策，若缺乏对复杂系统的充分了解，可能意外地适得其反。对于阅读和词汇学习者而言，这个故事提供了因果关系的绝佳范例：1935 年的一次引进如何波及数十年，重塑了一个大陆的生态。随着科学家继续研究蟾蜍非凡的适应能力，一个教训格外醒目——敬畏自然，三思而后行。
