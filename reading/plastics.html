The Development of Plastics: From Ivory to the Age of Polymers

塑料发展史 · 从替代象牙到聚合物时代

1

Plastics are everywhere — in the objects we hold, the packaging we unwrap and the bottles we drink from. Yet for most of human history no plastic existed at all: people relied on natural materials such as wood, stone, bone and metal, shaping these resources with hand and fire. The story of how a synthetic substance came to replace ivory, transform daily life and finally threaten the environment is one of the most significant revolutions of the industrial age — a revolution built on chemistry, demand and a single idea: the molecules, whose consequences few could foresee.

1

塑料无处不在——在我们握着的物品里，在我们拆开的包装里，在我们喝水的瓶子里。然而在人类历史的大部分时间里，根本不存在塑料：人们依靠木头、石头、骨头和金属等天然材料，用双手和火塑造这些资源。一种合成物质如何取代象牙、改变日常生活、最终威胁环境，这是工业时代最伟大的革命之一——一场建立在化学、需求和一个简单观念之上的革命：分子。

2

The turning point came from a shortage. By the mid-1800s, elephant ivory was running out, while demand for billiard balls had expanded and obtaining enough ivory had become difficult and costly, so manufacturers desperately needed an alternative. In 1869 an American printer, John Wesley Hyatt, combined cellulose from cotton with camphor and produced a hard, shiny, flexible substance that could be moulded and polished like ivory. He called it celluloid, and although it was made from a natural material, it was the first substance that could be shaped and reproduced cheaply — the ancestor of all modern plastics. Celluloid soon appeared in combs, collars and early films, but it was highly flammable and could never fully replace ivory.

2

转折点来自一场短缺。19 世纪中叶，大象象牙即将耗尽，台球制造商急需替代品。1869 年，美国印刷工约翰·韦斯利·海厄特将棉花中的纤维素与樟脑混合，制成一种坚硬、闪亮、柔韧的物质，可以像象牙一样塑形和抛光。他称之为赛璐珞。虽然它由天然材料制成，却是第一种能被廉价塑形和复制的物质——一切现代塑料的祖先。赛璐珞很快出现在梳子、衣领和早期胶片中，但它极易燃烧，永远无法完全取代象牙。

3

The real breakthrough came in 1907, when the Belgian chemist Leo Baekeland produced a hard, dark resin by heating phenol and formaldehyde under pressure. He called it Bakelite, and it was the first fully synthetic plastic: its properties — heat resistance, electrical insulation, and the ability to be cast in moulds — made it crucial for the new world of telephones, radios and electric wires. Bakelite demonstrated that chemists could design materials with precise control over their properties, and it revealed the potential of synthetic materials, opening the door to the plastic age. Moreover, because it could be produced in any colour and shape, it appealed to consumers who wanted cheap, attractive objects for the home, and mass production promoted its spread.

3

真正的突破发生在 1907 年。比利时化学家利奥·贝克兰在压力下加热苯酚和甲醛，制得一种坚硬深色的树脂。他称之为电木。这是第一种完全合成的塑料：耐热、绝缘、可铸模，这些特性使它完美适用于电话、收音机和电线的新世界。电木证明了化学家能设计出具有精确所需特性的材料，开启了塑料时代。而且，由于它可制成任何颜色和形状，吸引了想要廉价美观家居用品的消费者。

4

During the Second World War, the demand for new materials exploded. Nylon, invented in 1935, was used for parachutes and ropes; polyethylene, first made in small quantities, became vital for radar insulation; and Teflon found its way into the war effort. After 1945 these wartime materials underwent a transformation into civilian life, and plastic became the cheap source of bowls, toys and furniture, and above all of packaging — thin, cheap, disposable wraps and bottles that made food safer and shopping lighter. The uses of the new fibres varied from ropes to raincoats; Consequently, the world entered the age of mass consumption, and plastics became so cheap that it was easier to throw an object away than to repair it. As a result, a new word entered everyday speech: throwaway.

4

二战期间，对新材料的需求爆炸式增长。1935 年发明的尼龙用于降落伞和绳索；首次小批量生产的聚乙烯成为雷达绝缘的关键材料；特氟龙也进入了战争工业。1945 年后，这些战时材料进入平民生活，塑料改变了家庭：碗、玩具、家具，尤其是包装——薄而廉价的一次性包装和瓶子，让食物更安全、购物更轻便。世界由此进入大众消费时代，塑料变得如此便宜，扔掉一件物品比修理它更容易。一个新的词汇进入了日常语言：一次性。

5

Factors such as durability, lightness and low cost — the same qualities that made plastics useful — created a serious problem. Unlike wood or paper, most plastics do not decompose easily; a plastic bag can survive for hundreds of years, and a bottle may outlive its owner. Widespread waste plastic accumulates in rivers and oceans, and the mountain of rubbish shows no sign of decreasing, where it breaks into tiny pieces called microplastics that enter the food chain and harm wildlife. Nevertheless, the industry continued to grow, because the advantages of plastic — its strength, its cheapness and its ability to be sealed — made it hard to replace in medicine, transport and construction.

5

让塑料有用的那些特性——耐用、轻便、廉价——也造成了一个严重的问题。与木头和纸不同，大多数塑料不易分解；一个塑料袋可以存活数百年，瓶子可能比主人活得更久。塑料垃圾在河流和海洋中堆积，碎裂成微小的颗粒，即微塑料，进入食物链，危害野生动物。然而，塑料工业仍在增长，因为塑料的优势——强度、廉价和密封性——使它在医疗、交通和建筑领域难以被替代。

6

Why is plastic so hard to recycle? The answer lies in chemistry. Thermoplastics can be melted and remoulded again and again, but thermosetting plastics, once hardened, can never be softened; the two types must be sorted carefully, yet most household waste is a confusing mixture of many different chemical components. Converting the mixture into new plastic is costly: sorting, cleaning and reprocessing cost far more than making new plastic from oil, the sorting systems are rarely sufficient, and in many places only a small fraction of plastic waste is ever recovered, and the rest is burned, buried or dumped at sea. Thereby, the very durability that made plastic valuable has made it a burden on future generations.

6

为什么塑料如此难以回收？答案在于化学。热塑性塑料可以反复熔化和重塑，但热固性塑料一旦硬化就永远无法软化；这两类塑料必须仔细分类，而大多数生活垃圾是各种聚合物的混乱混合物。分类、清洗和再加工的成本远远高于用石油制造新塑料，因此在许多地方，只有一小部分塑料垃圾被回收，其余的被焚烧、填埋或倾倒入海。正是让塑料有价值的耐用性，使它成为后代的负担。

7

Scientists are now trying to design sustainable plastics that disappear. Biodegradable plastics, made from corn or plant starch, can be broken down by bacteria under the right conditions, though they may not degrade in ordinary soil or sea. Others are working on chemical recycling, which breaks polymers back into their original molecules so that they can be rebuilt, and on enzymes that eat plastic waste. In contrast, some experts argue that technology alone will not solve the problem: what is needed is not to eliminate plastic but a change of habit — fewer single-use objects, more reuse and refilling, and products designed from the start to be recycled. Notably, several countries have already banned single-use bags, and the results show that ordinary people will accept small changes when the reason is clear. Even a range of small changes, made by millions of people, would help.

7

科学家们正试图设计会消失的塑料。用玉米或植物淀粉制成的可生物降解塑料，在合适条件下可被细菌分解，尽管它们在普通土壤或海水中可能不会降解。另一些人则在研究化学回收——将聚合物分解回原始分子以便重建——以及能吃掉塑料垃圾的酶。相比之下，一些专家认为仅靠技术无法解决这个问题：需要的是一种习惯的改变——更少的一次性用品、更多的重复使用和重新灌装，以及从一开始就为回收而设计的产品。值得注意的是，一些国家已经禁止一次性塑料袋，结果表明，只要理由清楚，普通人愿意接受小小的改变。

8

The history of plastic is thus a lesson in the relationship between invention and responsibility. A material that began as a clever answer to a specific shortage of ivory became one of the most useful — and most problematic — inventions of the modern age. The challenge now is not to invent more — new materials generate new problems as well as new solutions — but to manage what we have invented: to reduce, reuse and recover, and to design a future in which the objects we make do not outlast the world that made them, preserving it for the next generation. In conclusion, plastic itself is neither good nor bad; it is a reflection of the choices we make, and the next chapter of its story is still being written.

8

塑料的历史是一堂关于发明与责任关系的课。一种作为象牙短缺的巧妙答案而诞生的材料，成为现代时代最有用——也最有问题——的发明之一。现在的挑战不是发明更多，而是管理我们已经发明的东西：减少、重复使用和回收，并设计一个我们制造的物品不会比制造它们的世界活得更久的未来。总之，塑料本身无所谓好坏；它映照着我们的选择，而它故事的下一章仍在书写中。
