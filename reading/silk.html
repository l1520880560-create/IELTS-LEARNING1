The Development of the Silk Industry: From Imperial Secret to Global Trade

丝绸产业史 · 从帝国机密到全球贸易

1

Few materials are more closely tied to human civilisation than silk. For nearly five thousand years this fine thread — woven into fabrics, garments and works of art — has carried trade, culture and power across continents. The story begins in ancient China, where legend says the Empress Leizu discovered — some say invented — the secret of the silkworm beside a mulberry tree whose leaves yield the finest silk. The fibre she unwound was strong yet soft, light yet durable, delicate and remarkably uniform; in Rome it was sold for its weight in gold. A material so rare and so valuable soon became a symbol of wealth and status, and the secret of its making became the most guarded property in the ancient world.

1

很少有材料像丝绸那样与人类文明紧密相连。近五千年来，这种纤细的丝线——织成织物、衣裳和艺术品——一直承载着贸易、文化与权力，跨越各大洲。故事始于古代中国。传说嫘祖在一棵桑树旁发现了蚕的秘密——也有人说她发明了养蚕，桑叶养出了最上等的丝。她抽出的纤维既强韧又柔软，既轻盈又耐用，细腻而匀整；在罗马，它按重量与黄金同价。一种如此稀有、如此珍贵的材料，很快就成为财富与地位的象征，而它的制造秘密成了古代世界最受守护的财产。

2

For centuries, China enjoyed a monopoly on this precious industry, and it guarded the secret of sericulture with severe laws. Anyone who revealed the secret of the silkworm, or who smuggled its eggs or cocoons out of the country, could be punished with death. The punishment was designed to prohibit the spread of the trade, to maintain a standard of quality that no rival could match, and to stop others from trying to exploit the imperial secret. Yet no law could fully protect it: the eggs of the silkworm travelled to Korea and India as gifts and as stolen goods, and each small theft slowly weakened the imperial grip on the market.

2

几个世纪里，中国独占着这项珍贵产业，并用严厉的法律守护养蚕的秘密。任何泄露蚕的秘密、或把蚕卵和蚕茧偷带出境的人，都可能被处死。这样的惩罚是为了阻止技艺外传、维持别国无法匹敌的质量标准，并防止他人觊觎帝国的机密。然而，没有法律能完全保护它：蚕卵作为礼物和赃物传到了朝鲜和印度，每一次小小的偷窃都在削弱帝国对市场的掌控。

3

The trade routes that carried silk to the West became known as the Silk Road. Along these routes, caravans of camels and horses moved across deserts and mountains, through oasis towns where traders exchanged silk for glass, spices and gold. The journey was long and dangerous: storms, bandits and broken supplies could ruin a merchant, but the profit on a single bale of silk was so great that the demand never failed — supply was the limiting factor. By the time the fabric reached the markets of Rome and Constantinople, its price had grown ten times over, while Rome retained an endless appetite for the fabric, and the imports of silk had begun to reshape the economies of the Mediterranean.

3

把丝绸运往西方的商路被称为丝绸之路。沿着这些路线，骆驼与马匹组成的商队穿越沙漠与群山，途经绿洲城镇，商人用丝绸换取玻璃、香料和黄金。旅途漫长而危险：风暴、盗匪和断裂的补给都能毁掉一个商人，但一捆丝绸的利润如此巨大，需求从未枯竭——供应才是限制因素。当织物抵达罗马和君士坦丁堡的市场时，价格已涨了十倍，而罗马对丝绸的胃口无穷无尽。丝绸的进口开始重塑地中海的各个经济体。

4

The turning point came in the sixth century, when two monks carried silkworm eggs to Constantinople hidden inside hollow bamboo walking sticks. The Byzantine Empire at last obtained the means to manufacture its own silk, and the new industry established itself under royal protection, its capacity growing decade by decade. From Constantinople, the method spread to Persia, then to the cities of Italy. In Venice and Lucca, guilds of weavers and dyers developed new patterns and finishes, and the quality of European silk began to rival that of the East.

4

转折点出现在六世纪：两位僧侣把蚕卵藏在空心的竹拐杖里，带到了君士坦丁堡。拜占庭帝国终于获得了自己制造丝绸的手段，新产业在王室庇护下建立起来，产能逐年增长。从君士坦丁堡，技艺传到波斯，再传到意大利诸城。在威尼斯和卢卡，织工与染匠的行会发展出新的图案与后整理工艺，欧洲丝绸的质量开始与东方匹敌。

5

Europe's silk industry expanded rapidly as demand grew. France, and above all the royal workshops of Lyons, raised standards of design to new heights: weavers combined silk with gold and silver thread, dyed it in brilliant colours, and produced fabrics that served kings and churches alike. The guilds controlled every stage of the craft — from the cocoon to the finished cloth — and their rules ensured both quality and profit for their members. A weaver's skill was tested by each pattern, and a single mistake could ruin a year of work and expose the weaver's failure to the whole guild; the profession demanded intense concentration.

5

欧洲丝绸业随需求增长而迅速扩张。法国——尤其是里昂的皇家工坊——把设计标准推到了新高度：织工把丝绸与金银丝线交织，染出绚丽的颜色，织出供国王与教堂使用的织物。行会控制着从蚕茧到成布的全过程，其规则既保证了质量，也保证了会员的利润。织工的技艺要经受每一个图案的考验，一次失误就可能毁掉一年的劳作，还会在行会里暴露织工的失败；这门职业要求高度专注。

6

The Industrial Revolution transformed the silk industry for ever. Power looms and new machines reduced the cost of production, and silk — once the most expensive of fabrics — began to reach the ordinary consumer. Then came the artificial fibres: rayon in the 1890s and nylon in the 1930s competed directly with the natural fibre, and the price of silk declined sharply — in some regions by as much as ninety percent, a decrease that closed entire towns. Cheaper rivals posed a direct threat; consumers could no longer ignore the difference in price, and many began to rely on cheap substitutes. The industry itself underwent a painful change, as mills converted to new materials and the widespread use of rayon reshaped everyday life. Yet the change was not only economic: the old association of silk with luxury slowly vanished, and the spread of cheap fabric altered the way people dressed.

6

工业革命永远地改变了丝绸业。动力织机和新型机器降低了生产成本，丝绸——曾经最昂贵的织物——开始进入普通消费者。随后化学纤维登场：19 世纪 90 年代的人造丝和 20 世纪 30 年代的尼龙直接与天然纤维竞争，丝绸价格急剧下跌——有的地区跌幅高达九成，整座城镇随之衰落。更便宜的对手构成了直接威胁；消费者再也无法忽视价格差异，许多人开始依赖廉价替代品。产业本身经历痛苦转型，工厂转而生产新材料，人造丝的广泛使用重塑了日常生活。但变化不仅是经济上的：丝绸与奢侈品的旧日关联慢慢消失，廉价织物的普及改变了人们的穿着方式。

7

Silk has survived, however, because no rival can reproduce its unique combination of strength and softness — a component of its appeal that rivals have never matched. Its feel remains relevant to luxury buyers, and silk thread remains crucial in surgery, where it is strong, flexible and safe in the human body. Artists still paint on silk panels, and designers still value its natural shine. In modern markets, silk has become a niche product again: sustainable farming, fair wages and the revival of traditional weaving have given it a new role as a premium material, preserving a craft that ten thousand years of change have not destroyed.

7

然而丝绸幸存了下来，因为没有对手能复刻它强韧与柔软兼备的独特组合——这一吸引力至今无人超越。它的手感对奢侈品买家依然重要，丝线在外科手术中依然关键：它强韧、柔韧，对人体安全。艺术家仍在丝绸画板上作画，设计师依然珍视它的天然光泽。在现代市场上，丝绸再次成为小众产品：可持续养殖、公平薪酬和传统织造工艺的复兴，赋予它高端材料的全新角色，守护着一门历经万年变迁而不灭的手艺。

8

The history of silk is thus a lesson in how goods, ideas and people move across borders — a story whose consequences are still with us. A guarded imperial secret became a global industry; a single thread tied together the economies of Asia and Europe; and the constant exchange of technology and taste transformed societies on every continent. Silk shows that significant change is rarely sudden: it derives from small acts of theft, patient craft and endless experimentation — and from the fundamental fact that people will always pay for beauty and quality. No specific fibre has yet offset its appeal; even the intense competition of the twentieth century could not eliminate it. Today the industry faces new questions about sustainable production and fair trade, and its future may depend on the same range of human choices — and on the potential of new ideas — that shaped its past. Whatever happens, the silk road of history will remain a witness to how trade promotes the spread of ideas, varying in scale from a single stolen egg to a world of commerce — and to how one culture can influence the destiny of all the others.

8

丝绸史因此是一堂关于货物、思想与人如何跨越边界的课——一个后果至今仍与我们相伴的故事。一个被严密守护的帝国机密变成了全球产业；一根丝线把亚洲与欧洲的经济联结在一起；技术与趣味的不断交换改变着各大洲的社会。丝绸表明，重大的变化很少是突然的：它源于一次次小小的偷窃、耐心的手艺和无尽的试验——也源于一个基本事实：人们永远愿意为美与品质付费。至今没有哪种特定纤维能抵消它的魅力；即便是二十世纪的激烈竞争也没能消灭它。今天，产业面临着可持续生产与公平贸易的新问题，它的未来可能取决于塑造其过往的同样一系列人类选择——也取决于新思想的潜力。无论发生什么，历史中的丝绸之路都将永远见证贸易如何促进思想的传播——其规模从一枚被偷的蚕卵到一个商业世界——也见证一种文化如何影响所有其他文化的命运。
