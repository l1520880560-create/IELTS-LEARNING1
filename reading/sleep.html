The Sleep of Hunter-Gatherers: What Ancient Lives Teach Us

部落睡眠研究 · 原始生活与现代睡眠的真相

1

Sleep is one of the oldest puzzles in biology. Every organism sleeps, yet scientists still disagree about why; every organ of the human body spends roughly a third of its life lying still, with little activity, and a single night without rest can dull attention, weaken memory, strain the brain and distress the mind and our mental health. However, for much of history, researchers studied sleep mainly in laboratories, and the results came from a small, unrepresentative group: modern city people who sleep in quiet rooms on soft beds. Consequently, what we call normal sleep — eight solid hours in one long stretch — may not be normal at all. The more we learn about the natural world, the clearer it becomes that human sleep was shaped by environments far older than electric light, and that to understand our own nocturnal nights we must first observe how people sleep when no artificial force influences them. The puzzle is not why we sleep, but why we came to believe that our own, industrial style of sleep is the only healthy kind.

1

睡眠是生物学最古老的谜题之一。每一种动物都睡觉，但科学家至今争论不休：人的身体把大约三分之一的生命用于躺着不动，而仅仅一夜不睡就会让注意力迟钝、记忆受损、心神不宁。然而，历史上的大部分时间里，研究人员主要是在实验室里研究睡眠，得出的结论来自一小群并不具有代表性的现代城市人——他们睡在安静的房间里、柔软的床上。因此，我们所谓的"正常睡眠"——八小时整觉、一夜长睡——也许根本不正常。我们对自然世界了解得越多，就越清楚人类的睡眠是由远比电灯古老的生存环境塑造的；要理解我们自己的夜晚，必须首先观察人们在没有任何人造力量干扰时是如何入睡的。谜题不在于我们为什么要睡觉，而在于我们为什么会相信，自己那种工业化风格的睡眠才是唯一健康的方式。

2

A landmark study decided to test this assumption in the most direct way: it travelled to three aboriginal groups of hunter-gatherers who inhabit the land and share it with wildlife who still live as our ancestors did — the tribes of Hadza in Tanzania, the San in Namibia and the Tsimane in Bolivia. For more than a thousand nights, researchers record the sleep of nearly a hundred volunteers with small wrist devices that measure movement and light, asking a simple question: how do humans actually sleep when no alarm clock, no electric light, no flashlights and no social schedule or routine forces them into a pattern? The choice was deliberate: these communities have no electricity, no permanent buildings, no household appliances and no written timetable, so their sleep offers the closest available evidence of the sleep our species evolved to take. Moreover, because the three groups live in very different climates, the study could also compare how temperature and daylight shape the sleep of people who never see a screen after dark — a rare chance to study sleep without the influence of technology.

2

一项里程碑式的研究决定以最直接的方式检验这个假设：它前往三个仍然过着祖先式生活的狩猎采集群体——坦桑尼亚的哈扎部落、纳米比亚的桑人和玻利维亚的齐马内人。在一千多个夜晚里，研究人员用测量动作和光线的小型腕带设备记录近百名志愿者的睡眠，只问一个简单问题：在没有闹钟、没有电灯、没有任何社会时间表强加节奏的情况下，人类到底怎样睡觉？这样的选择是刻意的：这些社区没有电、没有永久建筑、也没有书面时间表，因此他们的睡眠为人类物种进化而来的睡眠方式提供了最接近的证据。而且，由于三个群体生活在截然不同的气候中，研究还能比较温度和昼夜如何塑造那些天黑后从不看屏幕的人们的睡眠——这是一个在没有技术影响下研究睡眠的难得机会。

3

The findings overturned the common belief that people in the past slept longer than we do. On average, the three groups slept between 5.7 and 7.1 hours a night — less than the eight hours so often recommended, and roughly the same as people in industrial societies. They went to bed later in winter, slept less in summer, and almost never took a nap or a siesta in the daytime. Notably, there was no evidence that they suffered from insomnia or woke feeling tired; instead, most volunteers woke up feeling refreshed and energetic and rested, even after a slightly shorter, overnight night. The results were so consistent across three continents and across the full annual cycle that the researchers concluded that short sleep is not a disease of the modern world but a fundamental feature, part of the framework of human nature, and that the eight-hour night is closer to a cultural habit than to a biological law. The finding did not come from a small sample of unusual people; it held across hundreds of individuals, three populations and thousands of recorded nights.

3

研究结果推翻了"过去的人比我们睡得更久"的普遍看法。平均而言，这三个群体每晚睡 5.7 到 7.1 小时——少于人们常推荐的八小时，与工业社会的人大致相同。他们冬天睡得晚、夏天睡得少，白天几乎从不午睡。值得注意的是，没有任何证据表明他们患有失眠或醒来时感到疲惫；相反，大多数志愿者即使睡得更短，醒来也神清气爽。结果在三大洲如此一致，以至于研究人员得出结论：短睡眠不是现代世界的疾病，而是人类天性的根本特征；八小时整夜更接近一种文化习惯，而非生物法则。这一发现并非来自少数特殊样本，而是在数百个个体、数千个记录之夜中反复成立。

4

Perhaps the most surprising result was the absence of the two sleep problems that chronically trouble modern societies. None of the volunteers showed the classic signs of insomnia — difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep — even though they slept on the ground with no shelter beyond a thin blanket, exposed to heat, cold, noise and insects. Nor did they doze or nap: across hundreds of nights, daytime sleep made up less than a tenth of all sleep. And their sleep was not continuous, and it was no nightmare; like our ancestors, they woke, sometimes startled, several times during the night, often for more than an hour at a time. Accordingly, the team rejected the modern medical view that any waking in the middle of the night is a disorder: for a hunter-gatherer, a brief moment of alertness and arousal between cycles was simply part of a system of natural rhythms — a kind of peace, a time to adjust position, check the fire, or listen to the sounds of the dark and the silence between them. Sleep, in short, was not a single block but a series of lighter and deeper phases.

4

也许最令人惊讶的结果是，困扰现代社会的两大睡眠问题在这里完全缺席。没有一个志愿者表现出典型的失眠症状——难以入睡或难以维持睡眠——尽管他们就睡在地上，只有一条薄毯子，还要忍受炎热、寒冷、噪音和昆虫。他们也不午睡：在几百个夜晚里，白天睡眠占全部睡眠的不到十分之一。而且他们的睡眠并不连续；和祖先一样，他们夜里会醒来好几次，有时一次超过一小时。因此，研究团队否定了"夜里醒来就是紊乱"的现代医学观点：对狩猎采集者来说，周期之间短暂的清醒本来就是自然节奏的一部分，是调整姿势、查看火堆、倾听黑暗里声响的时刻。简言之，睡眠不是一个整块，而是一系列深浅交替的阶段。

5

The researchers soon noticed that sleep in these communities was fragmented — broken into several shorter blocks rather than one long stretch. This pattern was not a failure but a design: the body wakes when the temperature falls, and the coldest hours of the night are the most natural time to stir. In the desert, the rainforest and other harsh conditions, the night is rarely comfortable, and a sleeper who stays warm by adjusting position and moving is safer and healthier than one who lies still. Nevertheless, the most striking conclusion, of great significance, concerned temperature itself: it was the strongest signal controlling when people fell asleep and when they woke, and it worked in the opposite direction from what many scientists perceived. Yet the finding made perfect sense, to the delight of the team,, because a body that can respond to the cold by waking and moving is a body designed for survival in a habitat without without heating — an adapt that has shaped human sleep for hundreds of thousands of years.

5

研究人员很快注意到，这些社区的睡眠是碎片化的——分成几段较短的睡眠，而不是一整段长睡。这种模式不是缺陷，而是一种设计：身体在温度下降时醒来，夜里最冷的时候是最自然的清醒时刻。在沙漠和森林里，夜晚很少舒适，通过调整姿势和活动来保暖的睡眠者，比躺着不动的人更安全、更健康。然而，最引人注目的结论恰恰与温度有关：温度是控制人们何时入睡、何时醒来的最强信号，而且它的作用方向与许多科学家的预期相反。但这个发现完全讲得通，因为一个能通过醒来和移动来应对寒冷的身体，正是为在没有取暖的世界里生存而设计的——这种适应已经塑造了人类睡眠数十万年。

6

The finding makes biological sense. The human body is built to relax and cool down before sleep: blood moves to the skin, the nerves calm, the hands and feet become warm, and core temperature falls slowly through the night. Electric light delays this natural circadian rhythm, which is why people who stare at screens late at night, near midnight, often find it hard to sleep. In hunter-gatherer camps, by contrast, there was no artificial light to slow the body's clock; at sunset, as the fire died and the air cooled, people, after a day of hunting and exercise, began to yawn and grew sleepy, and they woke naturally at sunrise, when the sun rose, the sunlight returned and the warmth spread. Thereby the study revealed a simple mechanism at the heart of sleep, and natural melatonin follows the same rhythm: temperature, not time, is the body's oldest alarm, and a cool room may do more to restore our energy and muscles than any sleeping pill or drugs. The same principle explains why a warm bedroom feels so comfortable at first and yet so often leaves us restless and uncomfortable in the early hours.

6

这一发现符合生物学规律。人的身体在入睡前会降温：血液流向皮肤，手脚变暖，核心体温在整夜里缓缓下降。电灯延迟了这种自然节律，这也是为什么深夜盯着屏幕的人常常难以入睡。而在狩猎采集者的营地里，恰恰相反，没有任何人造光来拖慢身体的时钟；当火堆熄灭、空气变凉，人们自然产生睡意，太阳升起、暖意回归时便自然醒来。研究由此揭示了睡眠核心处一个简单的机制：决定睡眠的古老闹钟是温度而不是时间，一个凉爽的房间或许比任何安眠药都更能帮助我们恢复精力。同样的原理也解释了，为什么温暖的卧室起初如此舒适，却常常让人们在凌晨辗转难眠。

7

If this is how humans are designed to sleep, where did the eight-hour myth come from? Historians point to the industrial revolution: with electric light, factories, shifts and fixed work schedules, societies began to force sleep into one long nightly block, and any waking in the middle came to be seen as a disorder. Medical and psychology advice, in turn, turned this cultural habit into a rule, and generations of doctors taught patients that a healthy person sleeps straight through the night. The belief that such sleep is natural, the researchers argue, originated in recent centuries, a recent invention and a development of the industrial age, not a biological law — and they urge schools, hospitals and employers to reconsider schedules that punish people for waking at three in the morning, since the evidence shows that a broken night is simply how our ancestors always slept. The history of sleep, in other words, is written into our bodies.

7

如果人类天生就该这样睡觉，那么"八小时"神话从何而来？历史学家指向工业革命：有了电灯、工厂、轮班和固定的工作时间表，社会开始强迫睡眠缩进一个长长的夜间整块，任何半夜醒来都被视为紊乱。医学建议则反过来把这种文化习惯变成了规则，一代代医生教导病人：健康的人要一觉睡到天亮。研究人员主张，认为这种睡眠天然合理的信念其实是近代的发明，而非生物法则——他们敦促学校、医院和雇主重新考虑那些惩罚凌晨三点醒来之人的时间表，因为证据表明，夜半醒来本来就是祖先一贯的睡眠方式。换句话说，睡眠的历史早已写进我们的身体。

8

The lesson of these experiments is not that we should abandon beds, pillows and blankets and sleep on the ground. It is that sleep is far more flexible than the modern world assumes. What matters may be less the total number of hours than the quality of rest and the regularity of the pattern: waking briefly in the night is normal, a short sleep is not a failure and needs no cure, and the temperature and light of our rooms shape how well we restore our energy through restorative sleep. In an age of sleep deprivation, screens and stress, when calm feels rare, the study offers a quiet reminder that our bodies still carry the habits and traditions of our ancient ancestors — and that good sleep, like so much of nature, follows a seasonal rhythm that is older than civilisation. In conclusion, the science of sleep is only beginning, and the next decade may yield further surprises from the study of people who live as we once did; each one will remind us that to understand ourselves, we sometimes need to look back.

8

这篇研究带给我们的启示，不是让我们抛弃床和毯子、睡到地上。而是：睡眠远比现代世界所假定的更灵活。重要的也许不是总小时数，而是休息的质量和节律的规律性：夜里短暂醒来是正常的，睡得短不是失败，房间的温度和光线则塑造着我们恢复精力的方式。在这个充满屏幕和压力的时代，这项研究温柔地提醒我们：我们的身体仍然携带着远古祖先的习惯——好的睡眠，和自然界许多事物一样，遵循着比文明更古老的节律。总之，睡眠科学才刚刚开始，未来十年或许会从那些像我们祖先一样生活的人身上获得更多惊喜；每一次惊喜都会提醒我们，要理解自己，有时需要回头看一看。
