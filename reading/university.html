The Evolution of Universities: Nine Centuries of Learning

大学的演进 · 九个世纪的学习

1

The university is one of the most enduring institutions ever invent by human beings — older than most nations, shaped by revolutions, and still recognisable across nine centuries. It began in medieval Europe as a guild of scholars, a company of teachers and students joined by a common purpose: the pursuit of knowledge. That purpose proved so durable that the modern university still carrys its medieval bones — the lecture, the degree, the campus — beneath a skin of glass and silicon. To understand what universities are becoming, we must first understand what they have been.

1

大学是人类发明的最持久的社会机构之一——比大多数国家古老，被革命塑造，历经九个世纪依然可以辨认。它始于中世纪的欧洲，作为学者的行会，一群教师和学生因共同的目的而联合：追求知识。这个目的被证明如此持久，以至于现代大学依然带着它的中世纪骨架——讲座、学位、校园——只是皮肤换成了玻璃和硅。要理解大学正在变成什么，我们必须先理解它们曾经是什么。

2

The first universities grow out of cathedral schools in the eleventh and twelfth centuries. In Bologna, students organised themselves into a guild to protect their rights against the town; in Paris, it was the masters who formed the corporation. Both models produced the same invention: a formal institution that could grant degrees — certificates certifying that a student had mastered a field of knowledge. The medieval curriculum rested on two pillars: the trivium of grammar, logic and rhetoric, and the quadrivium of arithmetic, geometry, music and astronomy. Without printed books, teachers read aloud from handwritten manuscripts, and students memorised what they heard — a method that would survive for centuries.

2

最早的大学在十一、十二世纪从大教堂学校中生长出来。在博洛尼亚，学生们组织成行会，以保护自己对抗城镇的权利；在巴黎，则是教师组成法人团体。两种模式产生了同一个发明：一种能授予学位的正式机构——学位证书证明学生已掌握了一个知识领域。中世纪课程建立在两根支柱上：语法、逻辑、修辞的"三艺"，以及算术、几何、音乐、天文的"四艺"。没有印刷书，教师高声朗读手写稿，学生背诵所听到的内容——这种方法延续了几个世纪。

3

Survival demanded patrons. Universities depended on the church, the crown and wealthy nobles for money, buildings and protection; in return, they trained the priests, lawyers and clerks who run the state. The medieval university was therefore a strange mixture of freedom and control: scholars argued fiercely about theology while carefully avoiding anything that threatened the authorities. Yet within these limits, genuine inquiry flourished. The disputes of the schoolmen — sharpened by logic and fueled by ambition — created the habit of critical argument on which modern science would later build.

3

生存需要庇护人。大学依赖教会、王室和富有的贵族提供金钱、建筑和保护；作为回报，它们培养治理国家的牧师、律师和文书。因此，中世纪大学是自由与控制的奇怪混合：学者们激烈争论神学，同时小心避开任何威胁权威的东西。然而在这些限度之内，真正的探究蓬勃发展。经院学者的争论——被逻辑磨利、被野心驱动——造就了现代科学日后要建立的批判性论证习惯。

4

The Renaissance and the Enlightenment transformed the university's mission. Humanist scholars urged students to return to ancient texts and to question received wisdom; by the seventeenth century, the new science of experiment and observation begin to enter the curriculum. Galileo teach at Padua, Newton at Cambridge; the university became a bridge between inherited doctrine and direct evidence. The scientific revolution did not happen ''against'' the universities — it happened within them, in rooms where men argued about motion and matter with the same energy their ancestors had spent on the soul.

4

文艺复兴和启蒙运动改变了大学的使命。人文主义学者敦促学生回到古代文本，质疑公认的智慧；到十七世纪，观察与实验的新科学开始进入课程。伽利略在帕多瓦任教，牛顿在剑桥任教；大学成了承袭的教义与直接证据之间的桥梁。科学革命不是"反对"大学发生的——它发生在大学内部，在那些以先辈争论灵魂时的同样能量争论运动与物质的房间里。

5

The nineteenth century added a new model: the research university. The German scholar Wilhelm von Humboldt argued that universities should not only transmit existing knowledge but create new knowledge through investigation; professors should be free to pursue their own questions, and students should learn by participating in research. The idea spread rapidly — to the United States, to Japan, and eventually to every modern nation. The university gained a second mission: not merely to certify the learned, but to advance the frontier of what is known. That combination of teaching and research remains the distinguishing mark of the modern university.

5

十九世纪增加了一种新模式：研究型大学。德国学者威廉·冯·洪堡主张，大学不仅应传递现有知识，还应通过研究创造新知识；教授应自由追求自己的问题，学生应通过参与研究来学习。这个想法迅速传播——传到美国、日本，最终传到每个现代国家。大学获得了第二个使命：不仅认证有学问的人，还要推进已知的边界。教学与研究的这种结合，仍是现代大学的显著标志。

6

After the Second World War, the university opened its doors as never before. Governments recognised that a modern economy needed skilled graduates, and mass higher education begin in country after country. The student body diversifyed; new fields such as computer science and molecular biology spring up; funding flowed through grants, loans and scholarships. The campus grow into a small city, with hospitals, sports halls and start-up companies of its own. Critics warned that mass education would dilute standards; supporters answered that an educated population is the greatest asset a nation can hold — and that the right to learn should not be a privilege of birth or wealth.

6

第二次世界大战后，大学前所未有地敞开了大门。各国政府认识到现代经济需要熟练的毕业生，大众高等教育在一个又一个国家开始。学生群体多样化了；计算机科学和分子生物学等新领域蓬勃兴起；资金通过拨款、贷款和奖学金流入。校园长成一座小城市，有自己的医院、体育馆和初创公司。批评者警告大众教育会稀释标准；支持者回应说，受过教育的人口是国家能拥有的最大资产——而学习的权利不应成为出身或财富的特权。

7

Today the university faces its greatest test since the Middle Ages: the internet. Online courses offer free lectures from the world's best teachers to anyone with a connection; data and artificial intelligence change how research is done; employers question whether a degree is worth its rising price. Some predict the campus will disappear; others answer that the university has survived plague, war and revolution before, because it sells something no machine can replace: the slow, messy, human process of learning alongside other people. The lecture may shift online, but the purpose — forming minds that can judge, create and serve — remains the same.

7

今天，大学面临自中世纪以来最严峻的考验：互联网。在线课程把世界最好教师的免费讲座提供给任何有网络的人；数据和人工智能改变着研究的方式；雇主质疑学位是否值它不断上涨的价格。有人预测校园会消失；另一些人回答，大学曾熬过瘟疫、战争和革命，因为它出售的是机器无法替代的东西：与他人一起学习的缓慢、混乱、有人情味的过程。讲座可能会转移到网上，但目的——塑造能够判断、创造和服务的头脑——保持不变。

8

Nine centuries is a long test of an idea. The university has been called a relic, a factory and a refuge; it has been accused of serving the powerful and praised for liberating the poor. What it has never been is static. Each generation re-invents the institution to fit its own fears and hopes, and the version we know today will yield to something else. The deepest lesson of its history is not about buildings or degrees but about purpose: human beings will always find ways to pass knowledge from one mind to another, and they will always need places — real or virtual — where that passing can happen with honesty, rigour and care. That is a responsibility no technology can cancel.

8

九个世纪是对一个思想的漫长考验。大学被称为遗物、工厂和避难所；它被指责为强者服务，也被赞扬为解放穷人。它从来不是静止的。每一代人都重新发明这所机构，以适应自身的恐惧与希望，而我们今天所知的形式将让位于别的东西。其历史最深刻的教训与建筑或学位无关，而关乎目的：人类总会找到把知识从一颗头脑传给另一颗头脑的方式，也总会需要一些地方——真实的或虚拟的——让这种传递能以诚实、严谨和关怀发生。那是任何技术都无法取消的责任。
