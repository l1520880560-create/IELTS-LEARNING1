Fingerprints and Forensic Science: The Whorls of Justice

1

Few things are more personal than a fingerprint. Every person on Earth — including identical twins — carry a set of ridge patterns on their fingertips that is effectively unique, formed before birth and unchanged for life. This simple biological fact turned fingerprints into one of the most powerful tools of modern law enforcement. A single print left at a crime scene can link a suspect to a door, a weapon or a window; a single mistake can condemn an innocent person. The history of fingerprinting is therefore a history of identification — and of the danger of trusting patterns too quickly.

1

没有比指纹更私密的东西了。地球上的每个人——包括同卵双胞胎——手指上都有一套实际上独一无二的脊线图案，出生前就已形成，终生不变。这个简单的生物学事实把指纹变成了现代执法中最强大的工具之一。犯罪现场留下的一枚指纹可以把嫌疑人同一扇门、一件武器或一扇窗联系起来；一个错误也可以给无辜者定罪。因此，指纹术的历史就是身份识别史——也是轻信图案之危险的历史。

2

The use of prints to identify people is far older than police work. In ancient Babylon, clay tablets carried thumbprints to seal contracts; in China, official documents bear the prints of their authors. But it was not until the 1890s that scientists began to classify prints systematically. The British official Sir Edward Henry devised a system that sorted prints by the pattern of their ridges — loops, whorls and arches — into files that could be searched by hand. Meanwhile, the Argentinian police officer Juan Vucetich make the first positive identification from a murder-scene print in 1892, proveing that the method could do more than record identity: it could solve crimes.

2

用纹路识别人的做法远比警察工作古老。在古巴比伦，黏土板盖上拇指印来封存契约；在中国，官方文件带有作者的手印。但直到1890年代，科学家才开始系统地分类指纹。英国官员爱德华·亨利爵士设计了一套系统，按脊线的图案——箕形、涡形和弓形——把指纹归入可手工检索的档案。与此同时，阿根廷警官胡安·武塞蒂奇于1892年从凶案现场的一枚指纹做出了第一次确定无疑的身份认定，证明这种方法不仅能记录身份，还能破案。

3

A fingerprint is formed by the raised ridges of the skin, dotted with sweat pores and grooves between them. The pattern depends on nothing a person can control — it is fixed by genes and by the random pressures of growth inside the womb. Because the ridges renew themselves throughout life, the pattern remains stable even when the skin is cut or burned. This is what makes prints so reliable as evidence: a print left behind is a direct, involuntary record of contact — unlike an eyewitness, it cannot lie, and unlike memory, it does not fade. The criminal who wears gloves knows this; so does the detective who dusts a windowsill for the prints nobody thinks of wiping away.

3

指纹由皮肤隆起的脊线形成，上面布满汗孔，脊线之间是沟槽。图案不取决于人能控制的任何东西——它由基因和子宫内生长的随机压力决定。因为脊线终生自我更新，即使皮肤被割伤或烧伤，图案也保持稳定。这正是指纹作为证据如此可靠的原因：留下的指纹是接触的直接、无意识记录——与目击者不同，它不会说谎；与记忆不同，它不会消退。戴手套的罪犯知道这一点；在窗台上扫取没人想到要擦掉的指纹的侦探也知道。

4

Most prints at a crime scene are latent — invisible to the naked eye. Finding them is an art of chemistry. Powder sticks to the oily residue left by a fingertip; fumes of superglue coat prints on metal and plastic; a special lamp can excite the natural fluorescence of dried sweat. Once developed, the print is photographed, lifted and preserved as evidence. In court, an examiner then compares it with prints taken from a suspect, counting matching ridge characteristics — the points where ridges split, end or fork. Traditionally, courts expected examiners to find sixteen matching points before declaring a match, though the number varyes from country to country and has declined in recent decades.

4

犯罪现场的大部分指纹是潜指纹——肉眼看不见。找到它们是一门化学艺术。粉末会粘住指尖留下的油性残留物；强力胶烟雾能显现在金属和塑料上的指纹；特殊灯具能激发干涸汗液的自然荧光。显形后，指纹被拍照、提取并作为证据保存。在法庭上，鉴定人把它与嫌疑人的指纹比对，数出匹配的脊线特征点——脊线分叉、终结或开叉的地方。传统上，法庭期望鉴定人找到十六个匹配点才宣布认定同一，尽管这个数字因国家而异，近几十年来已经下降。

5

For most of the twentieth century, fingerprint evidence was treated as near-infallible — a standard against which other evidence was measured. Yet science gradually caught up with the claims. Studies revealed that examiners disagree with each other more often than the courts had assumed; that prints from different fingers of the same person can look strikingly similar; and that a partial or smudged print carries far less information than a clear one. The notorious case of Brandon Mayfield — an American lawyer misidentifyed from a fingerprint after the 2004 Madrid bombings — shocked the profession: three senior examiners had declared a match that was later admitted to be wrong. The error cost an innocent man two weeks in custody and exposed the gap between confident testimony and objective proof.

5

在二十世纪大部分时间里，指纹证据被视为近乎无谬——一种衡量其他证据的标准。然而科学逐渐赶上了这些主张。研究表明，鉴定人之间的分歧比法庭设想的更常见；同一个人不同手指的指纹可能惊人地相似；而一枚局部或模糊的指纹所含的信息远比清晰指纹少。布兰登·梅菲尔德案臭名昭著——这位美国律师在2004年马德里爆炸案后被一枚指纹错误认定——震惊了整个行业：三位资深鉴定人宣布了认定，后来承认是错的。这个错误让一个无辜者在羁押中度过两周，也暴露了自信的证词与客观证明之间的鸿沟。

6

The response has been a quiet revolution in forensic science. Courts now demand that fingerprint experts state their conclusions in statistical terms rather than as absolute certainty — how likely is it that this pattern would match by chance? Automated databases such as the FBI's IAFIS and its successor, Next Generation Identification, can search tens of millions of prints in minutes, and research programmes are building large data sets to measure the true accuracy of human examiners. Meanwhile DNA profiling — a far more powerful identifier — has taken the lead in serious cases, though it too raises new questions of privacy and probability. The modern forensic lab is a place of caution, not of easy answers.

6

回应是一场法医学的安静革命。法庭如今要求指纹专家用统计术语陈述结论，而不是绝对确定——这个图案碰巧吻合的概率有多大？美国联邦调查局的IAFIS及其继任者"下一代识别"等自动化数据库能在几分钟内检索数千万枚指纹，研究项目正在建立大型数据集，以衡量人类鉴定人的真实准确性。与此同时，DNA图谱——一种强大得多的识别工具——在重大案件中占据了领先地位，尽管它也引发了隐私和概率的新问题。现代法医实验室是谨慎之地，而非轻松答案之地。

7

The debate touches on something fundamental about justice. Evidence is never perfect; it is always probabilistic — a statement of likelihood, not of truth. The fingerprint is an extraordinary piece of biology, but it is examined by fallible humans and presented in imperfect courtrooms. The lesson of the last century is not that fingerprinting is useless — it remains one of the most valuable tools ever given to police — but that its value depends on how carefully it is used. A tool that can identify a burglar in seconds can also, in careless hands, destroy a life. The same ridges that prove contact can, misread, prove nothing at all.

7

这场争论触及正义的根本问题。证据从来不是完美的；它永远是概率性的——陈述的是可能性，而非真相。指纹是生物学上的非凡造物，但它由会犯错的人类检验，在不完美的法庭上呈现。上个世纪的教训不是指纹鉴定无用——它仍是警察迄今获得的最有价值的工具之一——而是它的价值取决于使用时的谨慎程度。一件能在几秒内识别出窃贼的工具，在粗心的人手里也能毁掉一条生命。那些证明接触的脊线，一旦被误读，就什么也证明不了。

8

In the end, the fingerprint teaches a larger lesson about evidence in general. Every branch of forensic science — from bite marks to bullet analysis to fingerprint comparison — has had to learn that confidence is not the same as accuracy, and that belief must yield to verification. The best scientists are the ones who can state what they do not know as clearly as what they do. Justice, like science, advances by admitting error and correcting it. The tiny whorls on our fingertips, unchanged since birth, may be the most intimate record a person leaves in the world — and they deserve the most honest reading we can give them.

8

归根结底，指纹教会我们关于证据本身的一堂更大的课。法医学的每一个分支——从咬痕到弹道分析再到指纹比对——都必须学会：自信不等于准确，信念必须让位于验证。最好的科学家是那些能把自己不知道的事和知道的事一样清楚地说出来的人。正义和科学一样，靠承认错误和纠正错误前进。我们指尖上那些出生前就成形、终生不变的小小纹路，可能是一个人留在这个世界上最为私密的记录——它们值得我们给予最诚实的解读。
