A Taller View: New Understandings of Giraffes

野生长颈鹿 · 重新认识被误解的巨兽

For centuries, the giraffe has been one of the most puzzling sights in the natural world — a creature so strange that early naturalists treated it as a freak of nature, a tall, silent giant whose neck seemed to contradict every rule of ordinary anatomy. However, the mystery is no longer simply why the neck is long, but what the animal actually does with its days and nights, and a wave of new research and discoveries — a quiet revolution — is revealing that much of what we thought we knew was incomplete. Observations of wild giraffes and other wildlife on the savanna now show a far more complex animal: one that hums at night, maintains close social ties, and arranges its sleep in a way no other large mammal does. Consequently, the giraffe is emerging not as a biological oddity but as a key indicator of the health of the African grassland and its environment, and its story is forcing scientists to revise long-held ideas about animal behaviour, evolution and conservation alike.

1

几个世纪以来，长颈鹿一直是自然界中最令人困惑的景象之一——一种如此奇怪的生物，早期博物学家把它当作大自然的畸形之作：一个高大、沉默的巨人，它的脖子似乎违背了解剖学的一切常规。然而，谜团早已不只是脖子为什么长，而是这种动物白天黑夜究竟在做什么；一波新的研究正在揭示，我们自认为知道的很多东西其实并不完整。对热带草原上野生长颈鹿的观察如今展示了一种复杂得多的动物：它会在夜间哼唱，维持紧密的社交纽带，并以一种其他大型哺乳动物都不会的方式安排睡眠。因此，长颈鹿正逐渐不再被视为生物学怪胎，而被看作非洲草原健康状况的关键指标，它的故事正迫使科学家改写关于动物行为、进化与保护的长久观念。

2

The classic answer to the giraffe's neck was simple: it evolved so that the animal could reach leaves and other vegetation above the heads of competitors, and Charles Darwin himself supported the idea that the neck lengthened through natural selection for browsing at the top of the tree through adaptation. Nevertheless, the theory has been challenged for decades, and the evidence has been mixed. In the 1990s, researchers point out that adult males use their necks as weapons, swinging the heavy head and ossified horns against rivals in violent contests known as necking, and some scientists claimed that the neck is chiefly a product of sexual selection: longer necks win fights, thereby attracting and winning mates, so they can reproduce. In contrast, a careful field study that examined wild populations in South Africa demonstrated that females — the sex that does not fight — possess proportionally longer necks than males when body size is account for, which supports the older foraging hypothesis: a longer neck lets females feed higher, sparing them from competition with males and smaller grazers on the ground. The truth may combine both forces, and the debate illustrates how one trait or feature can carry several adaptive meanings.

2

关于长颈鹿脖子的经典答案很简单：它进化出来，是为了让动物够到竞争者头顶的树叶；查尔斯·达尔文本人也支持这种说法，认为脖子是通过自然选择、为在树顶采食而变长的。然而，这个理论几十年来一直受到挑战。20世纪90年代，研究人员指出，成年雄性长颈鹿把脖子当武器，用沉重的头和骨化的角在被称为"甩脖搏斗"的暴力对抗中挥击对手；一些科学家由此主张，脖子主要是性选择的产物：更长的脖子赢得搏斗，从而赢得配偶。相比之下，一项对南非野生种群的细致研究表明，在考虑到体型之后，不参与搏斗的雌性拥有的脖子按比例反而比雄性更长——这支持了更古老的觅食假说：更长的脖子让雌性在高处进食，避免与雄性和地面上的小型食草动物竞争。真相或许融合了两种力量，这场争论也说明，一个特征可以同时承载多种适应意义。

3

Few claims about giraffes were more striking than the old assertion that they are hardly ever asleep. Early zoo keepers reported that captive giraffes remained standing all night, and textbooks concluded from zoo data that a giraffe sleeps for only about half an hour in every twenty-four hours, in short bursts of deep sleep. Modern studies adjusted the picture slightly: giraffes do sleep, but in a highly fragile, restless way — the deepest sleep lasts only a few minutes at a time, the neck curls back and the head rests on the hindquarters, and the whole episode rarely continues beyond an hour. The reason, researchers believe, is that lying down is dangerous and invites threat: a giraffe takes several seconds to rise, and during that time a predator such as a lion can easily overcome it. Calves, however, are adopted into an alternate rhythm; they sleep more and lie down more readily, secure in the knowledge that the adults around them keep watch and stay awake — a trade-off between rest and safety that shapes every hour of a giraffe's life.

3

关于长颈鹿，很少有说法比那句古老的断言更引人注目：它们几乎不睡觉。早期动物园管理员报告说，圈养长颈鹿整夜站立，教科书由此得出结论：长颈鹿每24小时只睡大约半小时，以短暂的深度睡眠片段出现。现代研究稍稍修正了这一图景：长颈鹿确实睡觉，但方式极其脆弱——最深的睡眠一次只持续几分钟，脖子向后弯，头枕在后臀上，整个过程很少超过一小时。研究人员认为，原因是躺下很危险：长颈鹿起身需要好几秒，而这段时间里，狮子这样的捕食者可以轻易制服它。然而，幼崽则被纳入了不同的节奏：它们睡得更多，也更容易躺下，因为知道周围的成年长颈鹿在放哨——这种休息与安全之间的权衡，塑造了长颈鹿生活的每一个小时。

4

For generations, the giraffe was described as a silent animal, and zookeepers rarely heard it utter a sound. Yet the silence may have been our own failure to listen. In 2015, researchers who attached recording and monitoring devices to captive giraffes captured a sound the species was never known to make: a low, humming that follows a clear pattern and lasts several seconds and seems to occur only at night. The hum has a fundamental frequency of around 92 hertz, within the range of human hearing but easily overlook when background noise is high. Moreover, the sound is often produced in a synchronized way, as if the animals are coordinating their calls and communicating, and the researchers suspect that the hum is a vocal signal used to keep the group together when visibility is low. The discovery underlines a simple lesson in interpreting animal signals: what we fail to perceive is not necessarily absent, and the quietest animals may be talking all the time.

4

一代又一代人把长颈鹿描述为沉默的动物，动物园管理员很少听到它发出声音。然而，沉默或许只是我们自己没能听见。2015年，研究人员把录音装置装在圈养长颈鹿身上，捕捉到这种动物从不为人知的声音：一种低沉的嗡嗡声，持续数秒，似乎只在夜间发出。这种哼声的基频约为92赫兹，在人类听觉范围内，但在背景噪音高时很容易被忽略。而且，这种声音常常以同步的方式发出，仿佛动物在协调彼此的叫声；研究人员怀疑，哼声是一种发声信号，用于在能见度低时让群体保持聚集。这一发现强调了一个简单的教训：我们感知不到的东西未必不存在，最安静的动物也许一直在说话。

5

Researchers reject the old image of giraffe society as equally misleading: a loose, random gathering of individuals with no lasting ties. Long-term fieldwork and careful observation have now overturned that view. Giraffes live in a flexible social network, a so-called fission-fusion system in which the group composition changes as individuals come and go, yet bonds between certain females remain remarkably stable over years. Researchers who study them identify the female as the core of giraffe society: related females and their young form the centre of a network, while adult males move between groups seeking mating opportunities. Even more surprising, female giraffes practise a communal-care tactic — calves are left with a few adult babysitters while their mothers forage, a behaviour involving elephants and some primates. Accordingly, social intelligence may be far more important to giraffe survival than the old textbooks assume, and a growing number of ethologists argue that giraffe society, they argue, belongs beside the more famous complex societies of whales and apes.

5

关于长颈鹿社会的旧形象同样具有误导性：一群松散、随意、没有持久纽带的个体凑在一起。长期实地研究如今推翻了这一看法。长颈鹿生活在一个灵活的社交网络中，即所谓的"裂变-融合"系统：群体构成随着个体来来去去而变化，但某些雌性之间的纽带却能在多年里保持惊人的稳定。研究人员认定雌性是长颈鹿社会的核心：有亲缘关系的雌性和它们的幼崽构成网络中心，成年雄性则在群体间游走，寻找交配机会。更令人惊讶的是，雌性长颈鹿会实行共同照护——母鹿外出觅食时，幼崽被留给几位成年保姆照看，这种行为与大象和某些灵长类动物相同。因此，社交智能对长颈鹿生存的重要性，可能远超旧教科书所假设的程度；越来越多的行为学家主张，长颈鹿社会理应在鲸类与猿类那些著名的复杂社会旁边占有一席之地。

6

No account of the giraffe is complete without its extraordinary body. The neck, despite being up to two metres long, contains only seven vertebrae — the same number as a human — each elongated and locked together by ball-and-socket joints; the whole structure is balanced by a powerful ligament running along the top. Pumping blood up that neck requires a heart of remarkable capacity: a giraffe's heart can weigh over eleven kilograms and beats at roughly twice the blood pressure of a human, while a network of delicate valves and a sponge-like tissue called the rete mirabile prevent the sudden rush of blood from damaging the brain when the animal lowers its head to drink. The legs are similarly engineered, with tight skin, thick bones and powerful muscles bearing the strain of a sudden gallop and withstanding its forces, and the spotted coat, unique to each individual, provides camouflage in dappled light. Every part of the animal, in short, is an engineering answer, built with the techniques of nature, to the problems of extreme size.

6

讲述长颈鹿，就不能不提它非凡的身体。脖子虽然长达两米，却只有七块椎骨——和人类一样多——每一块都被拉长，由球窝关节锁定在一起；整个结构靠一条沿顶部延伸的强韧韧带保持平衡。把血液泵上脖子需要一颗容量惊人的心脏：长颈鹿的心脏可重逾十一公斤，血压大约是人的两倍；而一套精巧的瓣膜网络和一种名为"奇网"的海绵状组织，可以防止动物低头喝水时血液的突然冲击损伤大脑。腿部的设计同样精妙：紧绷的皮肤和粗壮的骨骼承受着突然奔跑的张力，而每只个体独有的斑点皮毛在斑驳的光线中提供伪装。总之，这只动物的每一部分，都是对极端体型问题的工程学解答。

7

Despite the giraffe's fame, its future is surprisingly uncertain. Between the 1980s and the 2010s, the overall population in Africa fell by an estimated forty per cent, a decline so quiet that the International Union for Conservation of Nature did not list the giraffe as vulnerable until 2016, prompting warnings that the species was suffering a silent extinction, a slow death. The causes are familiar: expanding farmland, weather extremes, climate change, drought and shifting rainfall shrinks the savanna, while fences block ancient seasonal migration routes, and illegal hunting of wildlife for meat, leather and hides continues in several regions. Consequently, conservationists now urge a major and more active response — satellite collars track and follow the animals' movements, aerial surveys count remaining herds, and local communities are encouraged to protect, preserve and restore giraffes as a source of income through tourism rather than hunting. The giraffe, they argue, is not a single species but at least nine distinct subspecies or subgroups, each with varying population trends, and saving the animal means saving the whole mosaic of habitats, wilderness and landscapes it depends on.

7

尽管长颈鹿名气很大，它的未来却出人意料地不确定。从20世纪80年代到2010年代，非洲的总体数量下降了大约四成，这场衰退如此悄无声息，以至于国际自然保护联盟直到2016年才把长颈鹿列为易危物种，促使人们警告说，这个物种正在经历一场"寂静的灭绝"。原因并不陌生：不断扩张的农田蚕食着热带草原，围栏阻断了古老的迁徙路线，非法捕猎获取肉食和皮毛的行为在几个地区仍在继续。因此，环保人士如今呼吁更积极的应对——卫星项圈追踪动物的活动，航空调查清点剩余的种群，当地社区则被鼓励把长颈鹿当作旅游收入来源来保护，而不是捕杀。他们主张，长颈鹿并非单一物种，而是至少九个不同的亚种，各有各的种群趋势；拯救长颈鹿，意味着拯救它所依赖的整个栖息地马赛克。

8

The new picture of the giraffe suggests lessons far beyond one species. Its social bonds remind us that even simple herbivores can possess sophisticated behaviour; its hum challenges the assumption that silent equals passive; and its neck shows how a single anatomical feature can be shaped by several factors and influences at once. Moreover, giraffes contribute an ecological role that is only beginning to be appreciated: as they swallow seeds and move between trees, they act as gardeners of the savanna, and their presence keeps the grassland balanced. Protecting them, therefore, brings clear benefits — not merely kindness but an effective, practical investment in the health of an entire ecosystem — and one that depends on our willingness to look at a familiar yet unique animal with fresh eyes. The next decade of research, with new technologies, better methods and better fieldwork, may yield an increasing number of surprises that no one anticipated from the tallest creature on Earth, and each surprise will serve as a reminder that nature's most famous animals still hold secrets we have only begun to uncover.

8

关于长颈鹿的新图景所带来的启示，远远超出这一个物种。它的社交纽带提醒我们，即使是"简单"的食草动物也可能拥有复杂的行为；它的哼声挑战了"沉默即被动"的假设；它的脖子则表明，一个解剖学特征可以同时被多种力量塑造。而且，长颈鹿扮演着才刚刚开始被重视的生态角色：当它们吞下种子并在树间移动时，就像草原的园丁，它们的存在让草地保持平衡。因此，保护长颈鹿不只是善举，更是对整个生态系统健康的务实投资——而这取决于我们是否愿意用全新的眼光看待一种熟悉的动物。未来十年，随着新技术和更扎实的野外工作，地球上最高的生物也许还会带来更多惊喜，而每一个惊喜都将提醒我们：自然界最著名的动物，仍然藏着我们才刚刚开始发现的秘密。
