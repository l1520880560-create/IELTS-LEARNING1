Light in the Deep: How Creatures Glow

深海生物发光 · 黑暗中的生命之光

1

When the first deep-sea explorers lowered their glass chambers into the deep, cold depths beneath the surface of the open sea, they expected nothing but darkness. Instead they observed flashes of living light — blue, green and silver — swimming and drifting through the water like a slow firework display. The idea seemed so strange that many scientists reacted with open skepticism: how could a fish illuminate the darkness around it, or generate light inside its own body? Today we view bioluminescence, the term for light produced by living creatures, as one of the most remarkable tools of marine survival. What once seemed a rare curiosity is now known to emit from more than three quarters of deep-sea animals throughout the ocean, and for the visual senses of a hunter the single blue pulse may be the only visible signal for miles; the deeper we descend, the more common the glow becomes. From shore to open ocean, even the darkest waters are never truly empty.

1

当第一批深海探险者把玻璃观察舱降入开阔海面之下的幽深处时，他们原本以为只会看到一片漆黑。然而他们观察到的却是活生生的闪光——蓝、绿、银白——像慢放的烟花一样在水中漂移。这个想法太过离奇，许多科学家都公开怀疑：一条鱼怎么可能照亮身边的黑暗，或者在自己体内产生光？今天，我们把生物发光——活体生物发出的光这一术语——视为海洋生存最非凡的工具之一。过去被视为罕见奇观的现象，如今已知遍布整个大洋、存在于四分之三以上的深海动物身上；对于猎手的视觉而言，那一道蓝色脉冲可能是数英里内唯一的可见信号。我们下潜得越深，这种光就越常见。事实证明，最黑暗的水域也从不真正空无一物。

2

The secret lies in a chemical reaction that takes place inside special organs called photophores. When the substance luciferin combines with oxygen under the control of an enzyme, luciferase, the stored chemical energy is released and converted into light rather than heat — a transformation so economical it shrinks waste almost to zero. Many species delay the glow until needed and keep the reaction dormant during the day, but the right cue will trigger it instantly. Others, however, do not manufacture the chemical themselves: symbiotic bacteria are responsible for most of the glow, and the host fish simply gives them a safe home. This partnership, in which both partners rely on each other and neither is solely sufficient, is a striking example of how mutual dependence can stimulate evolutionary change and development, and promote the reduction of wasted energy. glow itself, in short, is a form of controlled chemistry, and every flash is a tiny act of metabolic engineering.

2

秘密在于发生在被称为发光器（photophore）的特殊器官里的化学反应。当物质荧光素（luciferin）在酶——荧光素酶（luciferase）——的控制下与氧结合时，储存的化学能量被释放并转化为光而非热，这种转化极其节能，几乎把浪费缩减为零。许多物种只在需要时才推迟发光，白天让反应保持休眠，但合适的信号会瞬间触发它。然而另一些鱼并不自行制造这种化学物质：共生细菌承担了大部分发光工作，宿主鱼只需给它们提供一个安全的家。这种双方都不能自给自足的伙伴关系，是相互依赖如何刺激进化、促进能源浪费减少的一个醒目例证。简言之，光本身是一种受控的化学，每一次闪烁都是新陈代谢工程的小小杰作。

3

Light serves the deep-sea hunter in many ways, and none is more dramatic than the anglerfish. From its forehead hangs a glowing lure, and fish that succumb to the glow are seized and consumed within a second. The trick is so successful that anglerfish reproduce in astonishing numbers, and their populations soar wherever the sufficiency of prey allows. Nevertheless, powering a light organ costs energy, and some species are reluctant to switch it on unless the reward is tremendous — one full meal may be worth a thousand flashes. The glow must also be enhanced with care: too bright a lure may attract the attention of a predator instead of a meal. In a world where every hunt is a contest of risk and reward, the anglerfish, a patient carnivore, has learned to breed patience into its very body.

3

光为深海猎手服务的方式很多，没有比鮟鱇鱼更戏剧性的了。它的前额垂下一个发光的诱饵，屈服于这道光的鱼在一秒内就被抓住、吞掉。这一招非常成功，鮟鱇鱼数量惊人地繁殖，只要猎物足够充足，种群数量就会飙升。然而，点亮发光器官是要消耗能量的，有些物种不愿轻易亮灯，除非回报巨大——一顿饱餐也许抵得上一千次闪光。这道光还必须小心增强：诱饵太亮，可能会引来捕食者而非猎物。在这个每一次捕猎都是风险与回报的较量世界里，鮟鱇鱼已经把耐心炼进了自己的身体。

4

Just as important is the use of light for camouflage. A predator looking up from below sees its prey only as a dark silhouette against the grey shade of the surface; many deep-sea fish therefore carry organs on their bellies that emit a soft, uniform glow, matching the downwelling light so precisely that the fish becomes almost invisible. Researchers trace this counter-illumination to a subtle, sensitive and flexible control system that adjusts the brightness from second to second — the constant tension between hunter and hunted made visible. In contrast, some squid go further: when under threat, they expel a glowing cloud that hangs in the transparent water like a ghost, and as the light fades, the predator is left with nothing to follow. The defensive tactic does not always work, but it tilts the odds in the prey's favour, buying the victim a precious moment to flee.

4

同样重要的是把光用于伪装。从下方仰视的捕食者，只能把猎物看作映在微亮水面上的黑色剪影；因此许多深海鱼在腹部携带发光器，发出柔和均匀的光，与下射光精确匹配，使鱼几乎隐形。研究人员把这种反照明追踪到一套精妙的控制系统，它分秒之间都在调节亮度——猎手与猎物之间持续存在的张力，就这样变得可见。相比之下，有些鱿鱼走得更远：受到威胁时，它们喷出一团发光的云，像幽灵一样悬在清澈的水中；当光渐渐消退，捕食者便无迹可循。这一策略并非总是奏效，但它让天平向猎物倾斜，为受害者赢得宝贵的逃跑时机。

5

Bioluminescence is also a language. Each species has its own flash pattern — a coded sequence of pulses, pauses and colours — and individuals use these signals to find a mate, steer the school away from danger or warn off a rival. In some shallow tropical seas, whole shoals of fish flash in perfect synchrony, creating a wave of light that rolls across the water. Some researchers reject the idea of deliberate teamwork and argue that the flash is not a symptom of intelligence but a simple instinctive response: each fish reacts to its neighbour, and the copies spread so quickly that the group appears to move as one — an explanation that may itself need revision. Whatever the cause, the pattern is enough to communicate a clear message, and those who remain suspicious of group intelligence should remember that in the deep ocean, communication is not a luxury but a necessity, and the collective behaviour of a shoal can rival that of any flock of birds.

5

生物发光还是一种语言。每个物种都有自己的闪光模式——由脉冲、停顿和颜色组成的编码序列——个体用这些信号寻找配偶、引导鱼群远离危险或警告对手。在一些浅海热带海域，整群鱼以完美的同步性闪烁，形成一道滚过水面的光波。一些研究者否认定这是刻意协作，认为这种闪光并非智能的症状，而只是简单的本能反应：每条鱼都对邻居作出反应，模仿传播得如此之快，以致鱼群看起来像是一个整体在移动——这个解释或许本身也需要修正。无论如何，模式本身足以传递明确的信息；那些仍怀疑群体智能的人应当记得：在深海中，通信不是奢侈品，而是必需品，鱼群的集体行为完全可以媲美任何鸟群。

6

The abundant range of glowing creatures is astonishing. Jellyfish float like lanterns; swarms of shrimp flick their lights to scare attackers; lanternfish cover their bodies with hundreds of tiny organs and make a nightly transit to the surface on the rising tide, ascending to feed against drifting currents; and the vampire squid, Despite its violent name, glows with a gentle luminous blue light. Deep-sea worms build glowing gardens on the seabed, and spider-like crustaceans hunt in the dark using light alone. Even on land the same chemistry appears: nocturnal fireflies court their mates with lamps, and certain fungi purify the forest floor while covering rotten wood with a ghostly green shine. Moreover, what unites them all — from the smallest microbe to the largest whale, a marine mammal and a vertebrate — is the same pair of chemicals, which suggests that bioluminescence has originated many times independently: a versatile trick, a permanent feature of life on Earth, and a striking example of nature's endless inventiveness. The deep sea remains sparsely explored, and every expedition reveals species unknown to science, adding fresh names to the long list of glowing wildlife.

6

发光生物的种类多得惊人。水母像灯笼一样漂荡；虾用闪光吓退攻击者；灯笼鱼全身布满数百个小发光器，每晚迁徙到海面觅食，向上浮升；吸血鬼鱿鱼尽管名字凶猛，却发出柔和的蓝色幽光。深海蠕虫在海床上建造发光的花园，蜘蛛状的甲壳动物仅凭光就在黑暗中捕猎。即使在陆地上，同样的化学也随处可见：萤火虫用灯求偶，某些真菌在净化林地的同时，给朽木盖上幽绿的光。而且，把它们联系起来的——从最小的微生物到最大的鲸——都是同一对化学物质，这表明生物发光曾多次独立进化：这一多用途的招数、地球上生命的永久特征，是大自然无穷创造力的生动例证。深海至今仍鲜被探索，每一次考察都会揭示科学尚不知晓的新物种，为长长的发光野生动物名录不断增添新名字。

7

Humans have been quick to exploit this natural technology. Researchers have isolated the gene that codes for luciferase and inserted it into other organisms, so that scientists can detect the activity of cells by watching them glow. The green fluorescent protein, first discovered in a glowing jellyfish, has become a valuable tool in medical research: by attaching it to specific proteins, doctors can observe the progress of diseases such as cancer in living tissue, and the microscope and other instruments have turned every cell into a functional window on the body's chemistry. Engineers have even cultivated glowing plants that could supersede electric lamps, an idea that may radically cut our use of electricity and modify how cities are lit at night; critics rate the technology as promising but untested, and Consequently an unbiased trial has yet to be done. For many researchers, the value of such work transcends the laboratory: it may thus one day revive the art of natural lighting, offer answers directly relevant to medicine, and transform how we study the living world.

7

人类很快就开始利用这种天然技术。研究人员分离出编码荧光素酶的基因，把它植入其他生物体内，于是科学家可以通过观察细胞发光来检测细胞的活动。绿色荧光蛋白最先发现于一种发光水母，如今已成为医学研究中宝贵的工具：把它附着在特定蛋白质上，医生就能在活体组织中观察癌症等疾病的进展，显微镜则把每个细胞变成了观察身体化学的窗口。工程师们甚至培育出发光植物，有望取代电灯——这个想法或许会从根本上减少我们的用电，并改变城市夜间的照明方式；批评者把这项技术评为前景广阔但未经检验，因此公正的试验尚未进行。对许多研究者而言，这类工作的价值超越了实验室：它也许有朝一日能让天然照明重获新生，为医学提供直接相关的答案，并改变我们研究生命世界的方式。

8

For all its promise, bioluminescence is under threat. Deep-sea mining, which targets rare metals on the ocean floor, could ruin entire ecosystems before we even know they exist. On land, artificial light pollution suppresses the signals of fireflies, and urban sprawl steadily expands to replace the wetlands where they breed. Conservationists defend the view that conserving these creatures is not merely a matter of scenic beauty or recreation: the same chemistry that lights a jellyfish may one day power sustainable lamps, restore the well-being of marine ecosystems, or purify polluted water, protect the fragile habitats that marine life depends on, and ease the pressure that mining increases on the marine environment. To abandon this research now, they warn, while the glowing world suffers, would be a reckless stride backward at a time when science is finally learning to see in the dark — and no decision about the ocean's future should be made without reference to the light its creatures carry.

8

尽管前景广阔，生物发光正受到威胁。瞄准海底稀有金属的深海采矿，可能在居民尚未被识别之前就毁掉整个生态系统。在陆地上，光污染压制着萤火虫的信号，不断扩张的城市蚕食着它们繁殖的湿地。环保人士主张，保护这些生物不仅仅关乎风景之美或娱乐消遣：照亮水母的同一化学，也许有一天能驱动可持续的灯具、恢复海洋生态系统的安康，或者净化被污染的水、保护海洋生物赖以生存的脆弱栖息地。他们警告说，在科学终于学会在黑暗中看清事物的时代，放弃这项研究将是一次鲁莽的倒退——而任何关于海洋未来的决定，都不应无视其生物所携带的光。
